# Domains and their briefs

The design is the owner's French note *maurice-domaines* (19 September 2026): one Maurice, domains that emerge from the conversations, each with a visible brief, proposed in a conversation Maurice opens, the garden seeded on consent. This note is the part of it that **exists in the code**, session by session of its roadmap. What is not here yet is listed under *Gaps*.

## What a domain is today

A row of `maurices` of **kind `domain`** — since the afternoon of 19 September 2026 the table holds no personas any more ([[maurice-personas-hats]] tells what became of them): a part of its creator's life that Maurice follows, with its name, tagline, statement (`prompt`), bound context, model and tools. The other kind of row, `companion`, is a **reading companion** — one book followed at the reading position, entered as a pinned conversation — and is not a domain: no brief, not in the prompt, not on the brief routes. A NULL `kind` reads as `domain`; the rows that predate the column were sorted once at the server's first start (a followed book → companion, the rest → domain), and the member re-sorts by hand in the editor or with `PATCH /api/maurices/:id { kind }`. A domain shared with a guest is the creator's domain, not the guest's (`domainsOf` in `services/domainBriefs.ts`, `isDomain` in `services/maurices.ts`).

## The brief

A short text Maurice keeps on the domain, two or three paragraphs, his working memory on it made visible. One row of `domain_briefs` per domain and member: `text`, `updated_at`, `sources_json` (the conversations the last rewrite read), `read_until` (the newest message it saw), `model` (what wrote it — the night's model, or `member` when the member did). See [[maurice-data-model]].

**Written at night** (`services/domainBriefs.ts`, 04:00 local, an hour after the corpus reconciles) or on demand: for each domain the server gathers what touched it since the last brief — the conversations bound to it, then the corpus's semantic search, then the full-text search on the name; eight at most — and asks the night's model (`domain_brief` invocation: DeepSeek V4 Flash, Mistral Small 3.2 in reserve) to rewrite the brief from the previous one and the excerpts. A first brief is ~300 words, an incremental one ~200; the output is capped at twice that, on a paragraph. Two rules in the prompt: nothing lent to the member they did not state, no question. The domain's own `prompt`, when it has one, is read as the statement of what the domain is about — its opening sentences join the semantic query, and the writer is told "the brief covers this, not the rest". A brief the member corrected is given to the next rewrite as *their* words, to be kept unless the new conversations moved things on. Every call is charged to the ledger's `system` spender under its own daily cap; the details of the night are in [[maurice-server]].

**Read in every private conversation.** The everyday Maurice's system prompt carries a section *Your briefs on {name}'s domains* with every brief of the member's domains, most recently rewritten first, under a global budget of **3 000 estimated tokens** (`briefsSection`, `BRIEFS_BUDGET_TOKENS`): a brief that does not fit whole is cut at a paragraph and closes the section, and the domains left out are named so Maurice knows they exist. The section comes **after the persona and the loaded context**, so the cached prefix only moves when a brief does — once a night, or on a correction — and before the tool roster. Two rules held in `services/claude.ts`: **never in a room** (more than one participant: another member would read them) and **never for another member** (the section is built from the briefs of the domains the turn's member created). The prompt tells Maurice a brief marked *in their own words* is the member's and prevails, to draw on the briefs without reciting them, and that the member can read and edit every brief in the app.

**Routes** (`routes/domains.ts`). `GET /api/domains` lists the member's domains and companions as two arrays — a standard member's own rows, the rows granted to a guest (`mine: false`, no brief) — each domain with `brief: { updated_at, model, sources }` or `null`, each companion with its `book_id` and the `conversation_id` of its pinned conversation (the most recently touched one bound to it that the member sits in) or `null`. The brief routes are the creator's only, 404 otherwise, and for a companion (*A reading companion has no brief*): `GET /api/domains/:id/brief` (the brief or `null`, with the domain's name), `PUT` with `{ text }` (the correction, stored with `model = member`; an empty text erases), `DELETE` (erase; idempotent), `POST …/brief/refresh` (rewrite now; `outcome` ∈ `written | unchanged | failed | capped`, 429 when the night's allowance is spent).

## How a domain is proposed

Since the night of 19 September 2026 (P2-B) domains **emerge**; the editor's *New domain* is the hand-made path that remains. Once a night (`services/domainMapping.ts`, 05:00, after the corpus and the briefs — the mechanics and thresholds are in [[maurice-server]]) Maurice takes each member's conversations that belong to no domain, has the corpus group them by their vectors (`corpus__map_conversations`, a second level cutting any big block), keeps the groups that recur over months and still live — eight conversations over four months on a large corpus, three over two on a small one — and asks the night model to name and describe each, in the member's language, saying whether it is one thing; a small grab-bag is cut by the model itself, which is how someone who talks little gets domains at all. When at least two alive domains emerge and no proposal is waiting, they are written as **proposals** (`domain_proposals`, [[maurice-data-model]]) and Maurice **opens a conversation** ([[maurice-chat]]) whose first message the same model writes: three domains that count now, the others that lived, the nuances it sees. A child never receives one, nor a guest, nor a member who received one within the household's days; a proposal ignored waits six weeks, then is put away and the night looks again.

In that conversation, and only there, Maurice holds three tools ([[maurice-tools]]): **propose** (list, show one in full, add one the member names), **adjust** (rename, merge, split, dismiss — a dismissed proposal's conversations never come up again), **adopt**. The prompt section of the conversation names the proposals and the rule: nothing becomes a domain without the member's explicit yes, and their words on what a domain is about are right by definition. **Adopting** creates the row of `maurices` — kind `domain`, created by the member, the summary as its statement, the three closest conversations baked into its context — binds every conversation of the proposal to it, and writes the first brief in the background from those conversations (the briefs service of P1-A, on the night model, under the `system` spender); the domain then appears in the app's list with its brief, editable like any other. The first real evening, on a copy of home's database: Paola (21 conversations) was offered *Santé et corps*, *Natation* and *Immobilier et réglementation à Bruxelles* for 1.2 ¢; she merged the first two into *Ma santé*, adopted it and dismissed the third in one turn, and the brief was on the page a minute later.

## Seeding the garden

Since the evening of 19 September 2026 (P2-C) an adopted domain can put **notes in the member's garden** — the "garden seeded on consent" of the design, and the first time Maurice writes a note nobody dictated. The rule that holds it: **a yes to the domain is not a yes to the notes.** After an adoption Maurice offers, once, to write a few notes on it; only when the member agrees to the notes themselves does he call **`domains__seed`** ([[maurice-tools]]), which exists — like the three others — only in the conversation the night opened, and stays there, after every proposal is settled, until the notes of each adopted domain are written or declined (`action: "decline"` records the no, so the offer is not made again). The prompt section of the conversation says all this; `domains__adopt` answers *no note written: offer to seed the garden and wait for a yes*.

What gets written (`services/domainSeeding.ts`): one call to the night model (`domain_seed` invocation, DeepSeek V4 Flash by default) on the sixteen most recent conversations bound to the domain — the same excerpts the brief reads, numbered, with the brief itself when it exists — returns a JSON the server turns into **one hub note** for the domain (a MOC: *what I understood*, *the open threads*, the topic notes as wiki-links, *where it comes from*) and **up to three topic notes** under it (`parent: <hub>`), each for a subject the model reads in at least two of the conversations. Every note is in the member's language (their locale, else the household's), under `notes/<locale>/`, its slug from the domain's or topic's title, stepping aside (`-2`) when a note of the garden already holds it; one git commit for the lot, pushed when the garden has a remote. **Every note carries its provenance and the mark of not having been reviewed**: `meta.opened: false` — the mark the articles pipeline puts on a fiche a share wrote automatically ([[maurice-knowledge]]) — with `meta.author: maurice`, `domain`, `domain_name`, `role` (`hub` | `topic`), `model`, `written_at`, `sources` (the conversation ids), and a *Where it comes from* section at the end of the body: *Written by Maurice on 19 September 2026 from 16 conversations, with deepseek-v4-flash-0731. Not reviewed yet: keep it, correct it, or throw it away* — then the conversations by date and title. The seeding runs inside the member's turn (their yes is a tool call on that turn), so it is **charged to the member**, under their own fuse — the night's `system` allowance is for what runs when nobody is typing. On the owner's corpus: 16 conversations in, four notes out, 25 to 40 seconds, under one cent.

**Reviewing.** The mark goes by a gesture of the member's: **keep** (the *Keep* button of the banner the [[maurice-web-garden|web garden]] shows on such a note, `POST /api/v1/garden-tools/review-note`, a string edit that drops the one line and commits), **correct** (rewriting the body through the garden tool's `update_note` clears it too; an edit in Obsidian is theirs to mark), or **throw away** (the banner's *Throw away* is the toolbar's delete). The app's garden list shows *to review* on such a note, and the domain's page says *N notes in the garden · M to review* with a way to the hub (`GET /api/domains` and `…/brief` carry `notes: { total, unreviewed, web_path }`). Absence of the mark is the reviewed state, so every note written before it existed is one.

## Bringing a history in

Someone who arrives with years of ChatGPT or Claude behind them need not talk with Maurice for weeks before a map exists: since 19 September 2026 (P4) the app's settings carry **Import my conversations** — a setting, not a step of the onboarding — and the import ([[maurice-knowledge]]) writes the threads as conversations of the member's, badged by origin, indexed for search, bound to no domain, so the very next night maps them. What the import had to settle for the nights: an imported conversation carries the dates of its export, and the briefs' `read_until` is a message timestamp, so a thread from 2024 imported today would never have been read into a brief; the importer now stamps `conversations.imported_at`, the briefs' night compares it with the brief's `updated_at` and reads such a conversation whole, once. Maurice may mention the import a single time, in the everyday prompt, to a member who never did it and refers to past conversations elsewhere; the sentence disappears once a history is in.

The **demo household** is built on this: `server/scripts/seed-demo.ts` writes Théo's history (`server/scripts/demo-conversations.ts`, CC0, written for the repository — bread, the balcony garden, Japanese, the bike that stopped, two one-offs, mostly badged as imported) so that the night after the seed, or `scripts/seed-demo-household.sh --morning` at once, Maurice opens the conversation proposing three domains: *the morning of the proposal*, tried on 19 September for 1.4 ¢ (three presented, the bike named as what lived). See [[maurice-commercialisation]].

## The domains in the app

The Maurice app (Mac and iOS) has **one Maurice** in its header and its send button, and a **domains list** (`DomainsViews.swift`) opened from a button beside *New conversation* in the sidebar — the Studio and the summon picker went with the personas. The list shows the member's domains, each with when its brief was last written and by whom (*Written by Maurice · 3d*, *In your words · 3d*, *No brief yet*), their reading companions, and *New domain* (Maurice Maurice sat there under *About Maurice* for a few hours on the 19th, before the documentation tool of [[maurice-tools]] replaced him). A domain of the member's own opens its **page** (`DomainBriefView.swift`): the mark, the name and the tagline; when the brief was last rewritten and by whom (*Written by Maurice · 3d · 16 Sept 2026, 04:02*, with how many conversations it read; *In your words* once corrected); the text in an editor; and the actions — **Save** (what the member wrote is what Maurice reads from the next turn on), **Rewrite now** (the outcome is said in a line: rewritten from N conversations, nothing new, allowance spent, failed), **Talk about it** (a new conversation bound to the domain, its baked-in context preloaded by the [[maurice-composer|composer]]), **Erase** (with a confirmation), **Edit** (the domain editor: name, tagline, kind, statement, context, model, tools). A companion's row resumes its pinned conversation or starts one; a domain granted to a guest opens a conversation on it (no page: the brief is the creator's). The greeting of a bound conversation shows the same mark and name and leads to the page and the editor. No brief yet reads as such, with the night and the button as the two ways to get one. Fifty-odd strings in the seven app languages.

## Ships vs. exists

The briefs — the data, the night, the prompt section, the routes, the page — the domains themselves — the kind, the sort, the list, the editor — the proposals — the mapping, the opened conversation, the three tools, the adoption — and the seeding — the fourth tool, the notes with their mark and provenance, the review in the web garden, the counts in the app — are in the server, the web garden and the Maurice app; nothing depends on the private tools: the corpus ships, and the mapping's arithmetic is in it. A hosted household without Ollama maps on its Scaleway-embedded store the same way (the tool reads whatever vectors the store holds). The hosted households run the same image and the same one-time sort at first start.

**In Carnet** (19 September 2026, [[maurice-carnet]]): a *Domains* shelf beside Books and Articles lists the member's domains with their brief's age and hand, their reading companions (each leading to its book) and the conversations Maurice opened; a domain's page shows the brief, editable (`PUT`), rewritable now and erasable — the same routes, the Grid's look; the book page shows and creates a companion (`kind: companion`). No *Talk about it* and no editor there: Carnet has no chat, and the domain's statement, context and model are the Maurice app's to edit.

## Gaps

- **Seeding is offered in the proposal conversation only.** A domain made by hand in the editor, or adopted before P2-C, has no way to ask for its notes: `seedDomain` takes any domain of the member's, but nothing calls it outside `domains__seed`. A *Write notes* button on the domain's page (a route, the same service, charged to the member) is the natural next step.
- **The seeded notes read what the conversations say, including the personal detail in them** — a hotel address, a medicine and its dose came through on the owner's trial. It is the member's own garden, private by default, and the mark asks them to read before keeping; whether the prompt should hold such detail back is a question for the first real reviews.
- **A seeded note is not in the corpus until the gateway's watcher sees it**: the server writes the file and commits, and asks nothing of the corpus (the garden tool pushes its own writes). The nightly reconciliation does not walk the file sources either, so a note lands in the semantic index when the watcher catches it.
- **The app shows nothing of a proposal but the conversation.** The proposals live in the server and in Maurice's words; there is no card, no list of what was proposed or dismissed, and Carnet has neither the conversation nor the tools (P3-C). The domain editor stays as the hand-made path and for correcting an adopted domain.
- **A big grab-bag is left aside.** A group the model reads as several things is cut by the model only up to eighty conversations; on the owner's corpus the block of conversations lived with Maurice (dreams, contacts, books, notes…) stays out of the proposals, said in the log, until the corpus's second level cuts it finer or the conversations are bound elsewhere.
- **The model's cut is not deterministic**: two runs on the same twenty conversations gave *Rénovation de l'appartement* once and *Natation* the next time. Acceptable for a proposal the member settles by talking; not a stable map.
- **The member's language is guessed** when they never set one in the app: the household's (the first admin's), else English. Paola's first opening came out in English before that fallback existed.
- **A reading companion's pinned conversation is found, not designed.** The row of kind `companion` holds the book and the conduct prompt; the list resumes the most recently touched conversation bound to it, and `GET /api/domains` names it. Carnet's book page now shows the companion and creates one (19 September 2026), but opening its conversation from the *book's* page — book preloaded at the reading position, the rules living with the book rather than in a row — is still the Maurice app's to build; a `maurice://conversations/<id>` scheme on the Maurice app would let Carnet hand over to it.
- **The briefs are the creator's.** A guest granted a persona has no brief on it, and a room never sees one. Whether a shared domain should have a shared brief is open.
- **The budget is estimated**, at three characters a token, like the rest of the context window; and the whole section is cached with the system prompt, so a correction in the afternoon costs one cache miss.
- **The rewrite's material is found by name and tagline** plus the prompt's opening; until the mapping gives domains with a real paragraph, a one-word domain still pulls in neighbours.
- **An imported conversation is read whole once, by the timestamp of the import, not by what it says**: a brief whose domain the import did not touch is still asked to look (the corpus search decides, eight conversations at most), and a very large import can crowd one night's material — bound conversations first, then the best matches, the rest waits for nothing. Acceptable for a first import; a per-conversation "seen by brief X" mark would be the finer tool.
- **The demo's eight personas are eight domains of Théo's** since the sort of P3-B, each due a brief at night; the seed is to be trimmed when it is next reworked.
