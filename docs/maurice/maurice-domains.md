# Domains and their briefs

The design is the owner's French note *maurice-domaines* (19 September 2026): one Maurice, domains that emerge from the conversations, each with a visible brief, proposed to the member — in a conversation Maurice opened, until 10 October 2026; in the app's list of domains since — the garden seeded on consent. This note is the part of it that **exists in the code**, session by session of its roadmap. What is not here yet is listed under *Gaps*.

## What a domain is today

A row of `maurices` of **kind `domain`** — since the afternoon of 19 September 2026 the table holds no personas any more ([[maurice-personas-hats]] tells what became of them): a part of its creator's life that Maurice follows, with its name, tagline, statement (`prompt`), bound context, model and tools. The other kind of row, `companion`, is a **reading companion** — one book followed at the reading position, entered as a pinned conversation — and is not a domain: no brief, not in the prompt, not on the brief routes. A NULL `kind` reads as `domain`; the rows that predate the column were sorted once at the server's first start (a followed book → companion, the rest → domain), and the member re-sorts by hand in the editor or with `PATCH /api/maurices/:id { kind }`. A domain shared with a guest is the creator's domain, not the guest's (`domainsOf` in `services/domainBriefs.ts`, `isDomain` in `services/maurices.ts`).

## The brief

A short text Maurice keeps on the domain, two or three paragraphs, his working memory on it made visible. One row of `domain_briefs` per domain and member: `text`, `updated_at`, `sources_json` (the conversations the last rewrite read), `read_until` (the newest message it saw), `model` (what wrote it — the night's model, or `member` when the member did). See [[maurice-data-model]].

**Written at night** (`services/domainBriefs.ts`, 04:00 local, an hour after the corpus reconciles) or on demand: for each domain the server gathers what touched it since the last brief — the conversations bound to it, then the corpus's semantic search, then the full-text search on the name; eight at most — and asks the night's model (`domain_brief` invocation: DeepSeek V4 Flash, Mistral Small 3.2 in reserve) to rewrite the brief from the previous one and the excerpts. A first brief is ~300 words, an incremental one ~200; the output is capped at twice that, on a paragraph. Two rules in the prompt: nothing lent to the member they did not state, no question. The domain's own `prompt`, when it has one, is read as the statement of what the domain is about — its opening sentences join the semantic query, and the writer is told "the brief covers this, not the rest". A brief the member corrected is given to the next rewrite as *their* words, to be kept unless the new conversations moved things on. Every call is charged to the ledger's `system` spender under its own daily cap; the details of the night are in [[maurice-server]].

**Read in every private conversation — as an index, since 20 September 2026.** The everyday Maurice's system prompt carries a section *{name}'s domains*: **one line per domain**, its name and a sentence, most recently rewritten first, under a budget of 1 200 estimated tokens (`briefsSection`, `INDEX_BUDGET_TOKENS`). The brief itself is loaded only when a question falls into a domain, by the native `domain_brief` tool (`services/domainTools.ts`), which takes the name the index showed and returns that brief whole. A domain with no brief yet is still listed: knowing it exists is most of what the index is for.

It used to be every brief in full, under a budget that went from 3 000 to 5 000 tokens in one morning. The measurement that undid that came the same evening: eleven briefs weigh **4 903 tokens** and they rode, entire, into a conversation about a family holiday in the Galápagos where not one of them applied. The budget was never the problem; everything travelling all the time was. The same eleven domains now come to **977 tokens**, and that is *before* any one-liner has been written — the index falls back to the brief's opening sentences, cut at a sentence end.

The one-liner is written by the night, in a **second call** made after the brief rather than as an extra paragraph asked of the first: the brief's own prompt is tuned, and a second output format is a good way to spoil it. It costs a few hundred tokens, fails softly (no summary means the fallback), and is **dropped when the member rewrites their brief by hand** — it described the text that was just replaced. `scripts/backfill-brief-summaries.ts` fills in the ones that predate the change.

Three consequences worth keeping in mind. The brief now arrives as a **tool result**, not in the prompt, so a night's rewrite no longer moves the cached prefix of every conversation the member has. The tool is **native, not MCP** — briefs live in the server's own database and the gateway has no business holding a member's working memory. And it is offered **only where the index is carried**, which is to say never in a room and never for another member: the two rules are held together in `services/claude.ts` by one flag, so they cannot drift apart. The prompt still tells Maurice that a brief marked *in their own words* is the member's and prevails, not to recite a brief, and to say when something comes from a brief rather than from a search just run.

**Routes** (`routes/domains.ts`). `GET /api/domains` lists the member's domains and companions as two arrays — a standard member's own rows, the rows granted to a guest (`mine: false`, no brief) — each domain with `brief: { updated_at, model, sources }` or `null`, each companion with its `book_id` and the `conversation_id` of its pinned conversation (the most recently touched one bound to it that the member sits in) or `null`. The brief routes are the creator's only, 404 otherwise, and for a companion (*A reading companion has no brief*): `GET /api/domains/:id/brief` (the brief or `null`, with the domain's name), `PUT` with `{ text }` (the correction, stored with `model = member`; an empty text erases), `DELETE` (erase; idempotent), `POST …/brief/refresh` (rewrite now; `outcome` ∈ `written | unchanged | failed | capped`, 429 when the night's allowance is spent).

## Out of the conversation (10 October 2026)

Everything below about *the conversation Maurice opens* describes how proposals reached the member until 10 October 2026, and is kept for the reasoning. That day it stopped. What a real case showed: a proposal existed nowhere but in that one conversation; the button beside *New conversation* listed adopted domains only, so a batch of conversations imported the day before, mapped that night and proposed as a domain, could not be found anywhere in the list; a conversation resembling an adopted domain was "left alone", neither bound nor proposed; nothing in the app bound a conversation to a domain any more; and a conversation wore a pastille only when the model happened to load a brief. Five changes, one piece of work:

**The proposals live in the list of domains.** The sheet opened from the button beside *New conversation* has a section **Proposals**, above the member's domains: every open proposal with its weight on five dots, its name, one line, its conversations and mail threads, how many were recent, and the day it was made — conversations and mail in one list, one decision per proposal. A row opens the proposal's **page**: the name and the paragraph, editable (the member's words prevail); a switch *Seed my garden with a few notes*, off by default; **Adopt**; **Merge** (tick the other proposals that join it); **Cut** (tick the conversations and threads that leave it, name the part: it becomes a proposal of its own); **Put away**; and what the proposal holds. A long-press on a row adopts or puts away without opening it. At the bottom of the sheet, **Put away (n)** lists what the member dismissed — and what the old six-week rule let lapse — each with **Bring back**: a proposal put away does not return by itself, the member can return to it. The routes are those of the drawer plus `POST /api/domains/proposals/merge`, `…/:id/split`, `…/:id/restore`, `GET …/:id` (the proposal in full) and `POST …/seen`; `GET /api/domains/proposals` now answers every open proposal of the member, the settled ones, and `unseen`.

**One sign, a badge.** The button that opens the list wears the number of proposals made since the member last opened it (`users.domain_proposals_seen_at`; `GET /api/domains` carries `proposals: { open, unseen }`). Opening the list clears it. Nothing else tells the member: no conversation, no message, no push.

**Maurice opens nothing and posts nothing.** The night writes its proposals (`conversation_id` null) and stops there; `domainOpener.ts` is gone, and so are the follow-up message, the "done, from the app" message after a validation and the announcements of seeded notes — the list shows what was done, the domain's page counts its notes. The guard on how often Maurice may open a conversation no longer concerns the nights (a child and a guest are still not mapped). **A proposal no longer expires**: it waited six weeks in a thread nobody read and was then put away so the night could look again; visible in a list, it waits until the member settles it. The four `domains__*` tools remain for a member who would rather say it ([[maurice-tools]]): they ride in **any conversation the member holds alone with Maurice** while a proposal is open — or for a day after an adoption, for the notes — with a short prompt section that tells Maurice the proposals exist, where the member settles them, not to bring them up himself, and that nothing is adopted without an explicit yes.

**Conversations are filed under the domains the member already has — by their neighbours' word.** A group the night model recognises as an adopted domain (`same_as`, or the same name) is no longer left alone, and an imported batch can no longer vanish into a group the model called a grab-bag: each conversation is asked about on its own. The corpus's `match_domains` centres the member's conversation vectors on their mean, takes the **twelve nearest conversations** among all of them, bound or not, and counts how many belong to one domain. Five of twelve bind a conversation of a recognised group; seven of twelve bind any conversation still attached to nothing and waiting in no proposal. Measured on the owner's store, each conversation left out of its own vote: seven of twelve finds 57 % of the bound conversations and is wrong on 0.8 %. The first idea — a centroid per domain, compared by cosine — was measured first and **does not work**: a domain made of a hundred everyday questions in French sits in the middle of everything said in French (a question on a flight's duration scored 0.82 against a health domain), and no threshold separates a conversation's own domain from the best domain of a conversation that has none. What the night binds is marked (`conversations.maurice_bound_by = 'auto'`), does not vote the following nights (a domain must not grow on its own guesses), is read once by the domain's next brief, and is **reversible**: the domain's page lists *n filed by Maurice*, each with *Detach*; a conversation the member takes out is marked `detached` and never filed again. A grab-bag that merely shares the name of an open proposal is no longer poured into it. Nothing is adopted this way: only domains the member already said yes to receive anything.

**Binding by hand is back.** `PATCH /api/conversations/:id/maurice` had lost its gesture. It now has three: the pastille's menu, the *Domain* picker in the conversation's details sheet, and *Attach / detach the current conversation* on the domain's page.

**A domain is recognised as the conversation goes.** Before the model answers, the server embeds the member's last turns and asks the same neighbours (`services/domainRecognition.ts`, one embedding and one vote, 1.5 s at most, then the turn goes on unmarked). Six of twelve: the domain is **recognised**, sent on the `tool_data` channel as `domain_recognised` and kept on the reply, and the app draws it as a pastille with a **dashed ring** — recognised is not read, and nothing is bound. Nine of twelve, and Maurice is told at the tail of the turn (beside the clock, where nothing cached moves) to read that brief before answering; on a first real turn about local models the domain came out at ten of twelve and the brief was read. On single turns of the owner's bound conversations, six is wrong on 3.5 % and finds about half; nine is wrong on 1 %. A tap on any pastille opens a short menu: *Open the domain*, and *Attach this conversation* or *Detach*. Only alone with Maurice, never in a room.

## How a domain is proposed

Since the night of 19 September 2026 (P2-B) domains **emerge**; the editor's *New domain* is the hand-made path that remains. Once a night (`services/domainMapping.ts`, 05:00, after the corpus and the briefs — the mechanics and thresholds are in [[maurice-server]]) Maurice takes each member's conversations that belong to no domain, has the corpus group them by their vectors (`corpus__map_conversations`, a second level cutting any big block), keeps the groups that recur over months and still live — eight conversations over four months on a large corpus, three over two on a small one — and asks the night model to name and describe each, in the member's language, saying whether it is one thing; a small grab-bag is cut by the model itself, which is how someone who talks little gets domains at all. When at least two alive domains emerge and no proposal is waiting, they are written as **proposals** (`domain_proposals`, [[maurice-data-model]]) and Maurice **opens a conversation** ([[maurice-chat]]). Its first message was, on the first real night (20 September 2026, 5 046 conversations, 63 groups, 15 proposals for the owner), three domains presented and twelve merely named by the model; since that morning (P2-D) the **server renders it** (`services/domainOpener.ts`): the model writes three short parts as JSON — the introduction, the nuances it sees, the invitation — and the server lays out, in the member's language, what a domain is and what it feeds ("a part of your life I follow closely… a short brief… which I read in every conversation you have alone with me: that is what lets me know who I am talking to. Nothing exists until you say yes"), then **every alive proposal** as one list item — its weight on five dots relative to the biggest, its conversations, its share of the member's conversations, how many were recent, the first sentence of its summary — and the lived ones named apart with when they went quiet. The fixed sentences stand in when the model fails, is capped or does not answer in JSON, so the opening never waits on it; it costs about a quarter of a cent. A child never receives one, nor a guest, nor a member who received one within the household's days; a proposal ignored waits six weeks, then is put away and the night looks again.

In that conversation, and only there, Maurice holds three tools ([[maurice-tools]]): **propose** (list, show one in full, add one the member names), **adjust** (rename, merge, split, dismiss — a dismissed proposal's conversations never come up again), **adopt** — and, since 20 September 2026, the member has a second door to the same acts, the app's drawer (below). The prompt section of the conversation names the proposals and the rule: nothing becomes a domain without the member's explicit yes, and their words on what a domain is about are right by definition. **Adopting** creates the row of `maurices` — kind `domain`, created by the member, the summary as its statement, the three closest conversations baked into its context — binds every conversation of the proposal to it, and writes the first brief in the background from those conversations (the briefs service of P1-A, on the night model, under the `system` spender); the domain then appears in the app's list with its brief, editable like any other. The first real evening, on a copy of home's database: Paola (21 conversations) was offered *Santé et corps*, *Natation* and *Immobilier et réglementation à Bruxelles* for 1.2 ¢; she merged the first two into *Ma santé*, adopted it and dismissed the third in one turn, and the brief was on the page a minute later.

**The mail as a source, 27 September 2026.** Once a member's mail has been read ([[maurice-knowledge]], the mail import), the night maps it too. What it reads of the mail is the **thread digests** the documents pass writes under *My mail* (`meta.origin: mail`, `meta.kind: thread`) — not the sealed messages, not the fiches of people, which are people rather than parts of a life. They are grouped **among themselves**, not with the conversations (`corpus__map_notes`, a group per twelve digests, a second level above forty): measured on the owner's store that evening, a digest's nearest neighbour is another digest at a median cosine of 0.76 and a conversation at 0.65 — Maurice's prose against the member's questions — so a common grouping sorts by style before subject, and an attempt to attach digests to the adopted domains by their vectors pulled half of them into *Santé et bien-être*. A thread's dates are those of its timeline (the note's own `date` is the day it was written); its thresholds are its own — four threads over two months, lived after a year quiet. At naming, the model sees the member's domains and the proposals still open and says, in `same_as`, when a group is one of them: the threads are then **filed under it** (`domain_mail` for a domain, whose brief is rewritten at once and reads each digest once from then on; the open proposal's `mail_json` otherwise) rather than proposed again. The rest are proposals like the others, carrying mail threads instead of, or beside, conversations; the opening, the drawer and the tools count them (*12 conversations · 30 mail threads*), and adopting one attaches its threads to the domain. **A conversation of proposals already open no longer stops the mail**: when new digests are there (a mailbox read the evening before), the night maps them, files what it recognises, and proposes the rest in that same conversation, with a message of Maurice's that follows the opening (`renderFollowUp`, no model call); nothing is opened, so the guard does not apply. The conversations still wait until the member has settled what is there. The mapping also waits, now, for the mail's night to end: a mailbox added in the evening can keep the reading busy until seven, and the digests are written at its end.

**Seen once, and never blocked, 28 September 2026.** The first night after the mail went in renamed the previous evening's leftovers — threads grouped but not named, or named as a grab-bag — into five near-duplicates of the proposals already there, and the owner read it as the same proposals again. Now what the mapping examined is remembered (`domain_seen`: a conversation id or a digest's path, per member): the groups are still made from everything unattached, so something new finds its kin, but a group is named only when enough of it is new — as many new items as a group needs, or half of it. A proposal that expires gives back what it held; a member mapped before the table existed was caught up once (the conversations older than their first mapped proposal, the digests older than their last mail proposal). And **open proposals no longer stop the night**, for the conversations either: what is new is proposed in the conversation that carries them, as a message that follows the opening; what the model recognises as an open proposal is added to it (its conversations or its threads); mail it recognises as a domain is filed under the domain, conversations it recognises as a domain are left alone — binding a conversation changes how it is answered, which is the member's to decide. Nothing to re-choose: a proposal is never proposed twice.

## The drawer: "Define my domains" (20 September – 10 October 2026)

*Replaced by the Proposals section of the list, above; the button under Maurice's message and the sheet are gone, the routes and the functions behind them are the same.*

Since 20 September 2026 (P2-D) the proposals are not only Maurice's words. Under the message he opened the conversation with, the Maurice app (Mac and iOS) draws one button, **Define my domains**, for as long as the server lists open proposals for the member — `GET /api/domains/proposals`, asked whenever a conversation Maurice opened is selected, so the button appears on the owner's existing conversation too and the state is never read off the text. It opens a **drawer** — a sheet from the bottom on the phone, a sheet on the Mac (`DomainProposalsSheet` in `DomainsViews.swift`) — with one row per open proposal, alive first: its weight on five dots and its numbers (conversations, share, recent; *quiet since* for a lived one), a box to **adopt** it, its **name and one line** editable in place (the member's words prevail), the night's *might be several things* when it said so, and a cross to **put it away** (undoable until applied). Only once a row is ticked does a second switch appear under it, **Seed my garden with a few notes**, off by default — one per domain rather than one for the lot, because a yes to the domain is not a yes to the notes (the rule of P2-C), the notes carry what the conversations say (a health domain and a violin domain do not deserve the same answer), and each seeding is a model call charged to the member. **Apply** sends everything in one request, `POST /api/domains/proposals/apply` — `keep` for a row only corrected, `adopt`, `dismiss` — on the very functions the tools run (`renameProposal`, `dismissProposal`, `adoptProposal`, `seedProposal` in `services/domainProposals.ts`): an adoption is the same domain, with its bound conversations and its first brief in the background. Then Maurice **says what was done in the conversation**, as a message of his own in the member's language (*Done, from the app. Adopted: X. The first brief is being written now… Put away: Y… No note was written in your garden*), fanned out over the room channel so the open thread shows it, and the notes asked for are written one domain at a time in the background, each announced in turn with its links, or its failure. The single routes exist too — `PATCH /api/domains/proposals/:id`, `POST …/:id/adopt`, `POST …/:id/dismiss` — for a client that acts one row at a time. The conversation's tools keep working beside the drawer ("adopt the violin" still does), the prompt section tells Maurice the member may settle things there and that the list it reads is always current, and a domain adopted without the notes box stays offerable in the conversation (`domains__seed`): an unticked box is not a refusal. Twenty-two strings in the seven app languages.

## Seeding the garden

Since the evening of 19 September 2026 (P2-C) an adopted domain can put **notes in the member's garden** — the "garden seeded on consent" of the design, and the first time Maurice writes a note nobody dictated. The rule that holds it: **a yes to the domain is not a yes to the notes.** After an adoption Maurice offers, once, to write a few notes on it; only when the member agrees to the notes themselves does he call **`domains__seed`** ([[maurice-tools]]), which exists — like the three others — only in the conversation the night opened, and stays there, after every proposal is settled, until the notes of each adopted domain are written or declined (`action: "decline"` records the no, so the offer is not made again). The prompt section of the conversation says all this; `domains__adopt` answers *no note written: offer to seed the garden and wait for a yes*.

What gets written (`services/domainSeeding.ts`): one call to the night model (`domain_seed` invocation, DeepSeek V4 Flash by default) on the sixteen most recent conversations bound to the domain — the same excerpts the brief reads, numbered, with the brief itself when it exists — returns a JSON the server turns into **one hub note** for the domain (a MOC: *what I understood*, *the open threads*, the topic notes as wiki-links, *where it comes from*) and **up to three topic notes** under it (`parent: <hub>`), each for a subject the model reads in at least two of the conversations. Every note is in the member's language (their locale, else the household's), under `notes/<locale>/`, its slug from the domain's or topic's title, stepping aside (`-2`) when a note of the garden already holds it; one git commit for the lot, pushed when the garden has a remote. **Every note carries its provenance and the mark of not having been reviewed**: `meta.opened: false` — the mark the articles pipeline puts on a fiche a share wrote automatically ([[maurice-knowledge]]) — with `meta.author: maurice`, `domain`, `domain_name`, `role` (`hub` | `topic`), `model`, `written_at`, `sources` (the conversation ids), and a *Where it comes from* section at the end of the body: *Written by Maurice on 19 September 2026 from 16 conversations, with deepseek-v4-flash-0731. Not reviewed yet: keep it, correct it, or throw it away* — then the conversations by date and title. The seeding runs inside the member's turn (their yes is a tool call on that turn), so it is **charged to the member**, under their own fuse — the night's `system` allowance is for what runs when nobody is typing. On the owner's corpus: 16 conversations in, four notes out, 25 to 40 seconds, under one cent.

**Reviewing.** The mark goes by a gesture of the member's: **keep** (the *Keep* button of the banner the [[maurice-web-garden|web garden]] shows on such a note, `POST /api/v1/garden-tools/review-note`, a string edit that drops the one line and commits), **correct** (rewriting the body through the garden tool's `update_note` clears it too; an edit in Obsidian is theirs to mark), or **throw away** (the banner's *Throw away* is the toolbar's delete). The app's garden list shows *to review* on such a note, and the domain's page says *N notes in the garden · M to review* with a way to the hub (`GET /api/domains` and `…/brief` carry `notes: { total, unreviewed, web_path }`). Absence of the mark is the reviewed state, so every note written before it existed is one.

## Bringing a history in

Someone who arrives with years of ChatGPT or Claude behind them need not talk with Maurice for weeks before a map exists: since 19 September 2026 (P4) the app's settings carry **Import my conversations** — a setting, not a step of the onboarding — and the import ([[maurice-knowledge]]) writes the threads as conversations of the member's, badged by origin, indexed for search, bound to no domain, so the very next night maps them. What the import had to settle for the nights: an imported conversation carries the dates of its export, and the briefs' `read_until` is a message timestamp, so a thread from 2024 imported today would never have been read into a brief; the importer now stamps `conversations.imported_at`, the briefs' night compares it with the brief's `updated_at` and reads such a conversation whole, once. Maurice may mention the import a single time, in the everyday prompt, to a member who never did it and refers to past conversations elsewhere; the sentence disappears once a history is in.

The **demo household** is built on this: `server/scripts/seed-demo.ts` writes Théo's history (`server/scripts/demo-conversations.ts`, CC0, written for the repository — bread, the balcony garden, Japanese, the bike that stopped, two one-offs, mostly badged as imported) so that the night after the seed, or `scripts/seed-demo-household.sh --morning` at once, Maurice opens the conversation proposing three domains: *the morning of the proposal*, tried on 19 September for 1.4 ¢ (three presented, the bike named as what lived). See [[maurice-commercialisation]].

## The domains in the app

The Maurice app (Mac and iOS) has **one Maurice** in its header and its send button, and a **domains list** (`DomainsViews.swift`) opened from a button beside *New conversation* in the sidebar — the Studio and the summon picker went with the personas. The list shows the member's domains, each with when its brief was last written and by whom (*Written by Maurice · 3d*, *In your words · 3d*, *No brief yet*), their reading companions, and *New domain* (Maurice Maurice sat there under *About Maurice* for a few hours on the 19th, before the documentation tool of [[maurice-tools]] replaced him). A domain of the member's own opens its **page** (`DomainBriefView.swift`): the mark, the name and the tagline; when the brief was last rewritten and by whom (*Written by Maurice · 3d · 16 Sept 2026, 04:02*, with how many conversations it read; *In your words* once corrected); the text in an editor; and the actions — **Save** (what the member wrote is what Maurice reads from the next turn on), **Rewrite now** (the outcome is said in a line: rewritten from N conversations, nothing new, allowance spent, failed), **Talk about it** (a new conversation bound to the domain, its baked-in context preloaded by the [[maurice-composer|composer]]), **Erase** (with a confirmation), **Edit** (the domain editor: name, tagline, kind, statement, context, model, tools). A companion's row resumes its pinned conversation or starts one; a domain granted to a guest opens a conversation on it (no page: the brief is the creator's). The greeting of a bound conversation shows the same mark and name and leads to the page and the editor. No brief yet reads as such, with the night and the button as the two ways to get one. Fifty-odd strings in the seven app languages.

## The icon, and the pastilles of a conversation

Until 3 October 2026 every domain wore the same closed book, and nothing in a conversation said that Maurice had drawn on one. Both changed that day.

**A domain has an icon** — `maurices.icon`, an SF Symbol name ([[maurice-data-model]]). It is chosen from a **short list** of fifty-four symbols (`services/domainIcons.ts`: health, sport, the house, money, work, code, a language, music, travel, family…), because a model asked for "an SF Symbol" invents names and a name that does not exist draws as nothing. The **night picks it** (`ensureDomainIcon` in `services/domainBriefs.ts`): every time a brief is looked at — the 04:00 run, *Rewrite now*, the first brief of an adoption — a domain without an icon gets one from the night model, which reads the name, the tagline, the brief's one-liner and the statement, and is told which symbols the member's other domains already wear. When the model is capped, fails, or answers off the list, **the words of the name decide** (French and English stems, the first word of the name outweighing the rest); when nothing fits the domain keeps the closed book and the next night asks again. The call is the `domain_brief` invocation, charged to `system`. **The member's choice prevails**: in the editor a tap on the mark unfolds the list, `PATCH /api/maurices/:id { icon }` stores it (any name shaped like a symbol, `null` to hand it back to the night), and a domain that has an icon is never given another. `GET /api/maurices` and `GET /api/domains` carry it; the mark (`DomainMark`) draws it in the list, on the page and in the greeting, falling back on the book when the system has no glyph by that name.

**A conversation wears the domains it mobilised.** Beside the back button — a glass capsule on the iPhone, a leading toolbar item on the iPad and the Mac — the app draws one round mark per domain (`DomainPastilles` in `DomainsViews.swift`): the domain the conversation is bound to, then each one whose brief Maurice read, in the order they came. Nothing new is stored for this: the `domain_brief` tool's result already travels on the `tool_data` channel and is kept on the reply ([[maurice-chat]]), and its payload now says which domain it was (`domain_id`, `icon`, beside the name — an empty brief included, since Maurice went and looked). So the pastille appears while the turn is still running and is there again when the conversation is reopened; replies older than the change carry the name only and are matched on it. Three marks on the phone, four elsewhere, the rest behind a *+N* menu. **A tap opens the domain's page** — the brief sheet described below, with its text, its date and its actions. Only the member's own domains show: the brief is the creator's, and so is the page.

## Ships vs. exists

The briefs — the data, the night, the prompt section, the routes, the page — the domains themselves — the kind, the sort, the list, the editor — the proposals — the mapping, the opened conversation, the three tools, the adoption — the seeding — the fourth tool, the notes with their mark and provenance, the review in the web garden, the counts in the app — and the drawer (the rendered opening, the proposal routes, the button and the sheet) are in the server, the web garden and the Maurice app; nothing depends on the private tools: the corpus ships, and the mapping's arithmetic is in it. A hosted household without Ollama maps on its Scaleway-embedded store the same way (the tool reads whatever vectors the store holds). The hosted households run the same image and the same one-time sort at first start.

**In Carnet** (19 September 2026, [[maurice-carnet]]): a *Domains* shelf beside Books and Articles lists the member's domains with their brief's age and hand, their reading companions (each leading to its book) and the conversations Maurice opened; a domain's page shows the brief, editable (`PUT`), rewritable now and erasable — the same routes, the Grid's look; the book page shows and creates a companion (`kind: companion`). No *Talk about it* and no editor there: Carnet has no chat, and the domain's statement, context and model are the Maurice app's to edit.

## Gaps

- **The pastilles are the Maurice app's.** Carnet lists the domains but draws neither the icon nor the marks, and the web garden knows nothing of either. They also say *read*, not *used*: a brief Maurice loaded and then ignored leaves its mark like any other, and a domain he spoke from on the index line alone, without loading the brief, leaves none.
- **The icon list lives twice**, in `services/domainIcons.ts` and in the app's `DomainIcons` — the editor needs it offline and the night needs it in the prompt. A symbol added to one and not the other is only a missing choice, not a fault. The keyword fallback speaks French and English only.
- **Seen in simulators, not on a device** (10 October 2026): the pastilles — bound, brief read — their menu, binding from it, the badge, the Proposals section and a proposal's page were run on an iPhone and an iPad simulator against a copy of the home database; the dashed ring of a recognition that reads no brief, merge and cut on screen, the Mac, and a paired device were not looked at.
- **No domain emerges from a big grab-bag, still.** Why the owner has no domain on the design of Maurice itself, found on 10 October 2026: its conversations sit in a group of two hundred the model reads as several things — it names that very subject in its hint — which is above the eighty the model may cut, so the group is left aside every night, now marked as seen; others went, by name, into a catch-all open proposal that had grown to several hundred conversations (that pouring is stopped; the proposal can be cut by hand in its page, twelve conversations at a time is not a tool for that size). A second level of clustering inside a group the model refuses, or a cut the model makes on a sample, is the missing piece.
- **The neighbours' vote needs conversations to vote.** A domain made by hand, with nothing bound to it, is never recognised and never receives anything; a member with fewer than thirty indexed conversations gets no vote at all. A new domain's first bindings are the adoption's.
- **What the night files is said nowhere but on the domain's page.** No count on the list's row, no badge: the member finds *n filed by Maurice* when they open the domain.
- **The domain tools ride in every private conversation while a proposal is open** — four tool definitions in the cached prefix of a member who settles nothing. Offering them only when the member speaks of domains was weighed and not built: a roster that changes between turns costs the cache and can vanish mid-exchange.
- **A proposal page cuts one part at a time**, and shows every conversation of a very large proposal in one list.
- **Carnet knows none of this**: it still lists the conversations Maurice opened, of which there will be no new ones for domains or mail, and has neither the proposals nor the pastilles.
- **A group named once is not named again** unless enough new material joins it; a leftover the model once called a grab-bag stays unnamed until then.
- **`same_as` is the model's word, matched on the name** — or, since the first real run the same evening (27 September 2026: 252 digests, 9 proposals, 27 threads filed under three open proposals, 8 ¢), a group the model names exactly like something already there, which it did once without saying so. A group it recognises under a slightly different name is proposed again, and one it wrongly recognises is filed under a domain it does not belong to; the brief reads it, the member does not see it anywhere yet (a domain's page lists its conversations, not its threads).
- **The opening message is long for a member with many domains** — fifteen list items for the owner, one line each: it reads on a phone, but a member with thirty alive proposals would get thirty; a cap with "and N more in the drawer" is the obvious next step if it happens.
- **Seeding is offered at adoption only** (the switch on the proposal's page, or `domains__seed` within a day). A domain made by hand in the editor, or adopted before P2-C, has no way to ask for its notes: `seedDomain` takes any domain of the member's, but nothing calls it outside `domains__seed`. A *Write notes* button on the domain's page (a route, the same service, charged to the member) is the natural next step.
- **The seeded notes read what the conversations say, including the personal detail in them** — a hotel address, a medicine and its dose came through on the owner's trial. It is the member's own garden, private by default, and the mark asks them to read before keeping; whether the prompt should hold such detail back is a question for the first real reviews.
- **A seeded note reaches the corpus through the gateway's watcher, not a push**: the server writes the file and commits, and asks nothing of the corpus (the garden tool pushes its own writes). Until 27 September 2026 the watcher ignored moves and the nightly did not walk the file sources, so the notes seeded on 20 September never reached the index; both are fixed ([[maurice-server]]), and a seeded note is searchable within seconds, the next night at the latest.
- **A big grab-bag is left aside.** A group the model reads as several things is cut by the model only up to eighty conversations; on the owner's corpus the block of conversations lived with Maurice (dreams, contacts, books, notes…) stays out of the proposals, said in the log, until the corpus's second level cuts it finer or the conversations are bound elsewhere.
- **The model's cut is not deterministic**: two runs on the same twenty conversations gave *Rénovation de l'appartement* once and *Natation* the next time. Acceptable for a proposal the member settles by talking; not a stable map.
- **The member's language is guessed** when they never set one in the app: the household's (the first admin's), else English. Paola's first opening came out in English before that fallback existed.
- **A reading companion's pinned conversation is found, not designed.** The row of kind `companion` holds the book and the conduct prompt; the list resumes the most recently touched conversation bound to it, and `GET /api/domains` names it. Carnet's book page now shows the companion and creates one (19 September 2026), but opening its conversation from the *book's* page — book preloaded at the reading position, the rules living with the book rather than in a row — is still the Maurice app's to build; a `maurice://conversations/<id>` scheme on the Maurice app would let Carnet hand over to it.
- **The briefs are the creator's.** A guest granted a persona has no brief on it, and a room never sees one. Whether a shared domain should have a shared brief is open.
- **The budget is estimated**, at three characters a token, like the rest of the context window; and the whole section is cached with the system prompt, so a correction in the afternoon costs one cache miss.
- **The rewrite's material is found by name and tagline** plus the prompt's opening; until the mapping gives domains with a real paragraph, a one-word domain still pulls in neighbours.
- **An imported conversation is read whole once, by the timestamp of the import, not by what it says**: a brief whose domain the import did not touch is still asked to look (the corpus search decides, eight conversations at most), and a very large import can crowd one night's material — bound conversations first, then the best matches, the rest waits for nothing. Acceptable for a first import; a per-conversation "seen by brief X" mark would be the finer tool.
- **The demo's eight personas are eight domains of Théo's** since the sort of P3-B, each due a brief at night; the seed is to be trimmed when it is next reworked.
