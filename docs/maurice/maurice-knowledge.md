# Knowledge capabilities

The knowledge tools are how Maurice reads and grows what the household *knows*. The garden and the corpus ship; tracks are experimental.

## Garden — the memory itself *(ships)*

`tools/garden/server.py` is the public capability. It is the full CRUD over the Markdown garden, all of it deliberate human action (Maurice writes only when asked):

- **Notes** — `create_note`, `update_note`, `get_note`, `list_notes`, `delete_note`, `set_image`, `toggle_public` / `toggle_private`. Frontmatter carries `flags` (public/encrypted/moc/…), `parent`, `tags`, `icon`, and more.
- **Journal** — dated **daily notes** (`write_daily_note`, `get_daily_note`, `search_daily_notes`) and **dreams** (`write_dream`, …), with entity-linking that turns named people into links to their fiches.
- **Fragments** — `append_fragment` and friends: collapsible sub-sections within a note, and the place an article's full text lives beside its fiche.
- **Resources** — typed entries for books, movies, series, podcasts, articles, games, music, and people. `search_*` then `create_*_entry` pull metadata and cover art from **Google Books, TMDB, IGDB, MusicBrainz, Podcast Index, Wikidata, and Open Graph**, downloading images into the garden. The metadata API keys are set from the admin dashboard. **Music** (since 8 October 2026) is filed by album: `search_album` / `create_album_entry` look up a MusicBrainz *release group* and take the sleeve from the Cover Art Archive. It needs no key, but MusicBrainz allows one request a second per IP, so the calls queue; when nobody picks a candidate, the original plain album is preferred over a reissue or a compilation of the same title. Not yet: music on the garden shelf (`garden/entries?view=shelf`) and in Carnet.
- **Fiches** — the working face of a media entry, see below. `open_fiche` fetches the metadata and opens one; `update_fiche` re-fetches it; `promote_fiche` turns it into a published card *when a verdict exists* — a card is the reader's verdict, not Maurice's to draft.
- **Imagery & publishing** — `generate_evocation` + `generate_hero_image` (via **fal.ai**) make banner art; `publish_content` and `deploy_site` push the garden to the [[maurice-web-garden|web]], and publishing is something you can ask for in a chat.
- **People** — `link_contact_to_person` / `find_person_by_contact` bind a garden person to a CardDAV contact.

Storage is git-backed Markdown under `~/.maurice/gardens/<username>/`, one repository per member — visible, ownable, diffable, and openable in **Obsidian** on the Mac or **Working Copy** on the iPad straight from the web garden's pages. This is the [[maurice|tamed memory]] made literal.

## A media entry has faces

Since August 2026 a garden entry for a book, a film, an article, a person is one *subject* that may exist as up to three files:

- the **card** (`<slug>.md`) — the published face, what the site renders: the reader's verdict;
- the **fiche** (`<slug>-fiche.md`) — the working face, "the back of the card": provider metadata under `meta:`, the reader's comments under `## Commentaire`, résonances under `## Résonances`, and beside it a `_fragments/` directory for long material such as an article's full text;
- the **flashcards** (`<slug>-fiche/_cards/`) — see below.

**A person's fiche is a hub** (27 September 2026, lot 3 of `specs/contacts.md`). `people/<locale>/<slug>-fiche.md` is where the mail pass writes about a person now, never a note: the frontmatter carries `status` (pending / confirmed / rejected — confirmed for someone in the member's address book or a fiche the member wrote), `carddav_uid`, `identities` (each address with the mailboxes it writes to, its status, `source: vcard | mail`, and a `conflict` when the mail contradicts the card — another name, two cards), and `relation` (status, `since`, `until`, sources, `written_hash`); the body opens with a `## La relation` section — who this person is to the member, once — and the interactions are **fragments**, one per address and mailbox, `origin: mail`, `status`, `mailbox`, `sources`, `written_hash`. **Touching confirms**: a fragment or a relation whose text no longer matches its hash was corrected by the member, is never rewritten, and a corrected relation is given to every later pass as a fact and sends that person's pending fragments back to be rewritten with it. An edit through the garden tool (`update_fragment`, i.e. Maurice) moves the hash with it and records `edited_by: maurice`: only the member confirms. A link the member marks `rejected` in `identities` is never made again. The member's own fiche keeps its title, body and marks; the relation section is put first. **The exchanges** (27 September 2026, lot 8): after the relation, a `## Les échanges` section the documents pass writes without a model from the header store — how many messages, since when, the last one, the twelve newest linked — rewritten whenever the mail moves; and `get_fiche` on a person returns its fragments with it, so reading the fiche is one call. **The gestures** (27 September 2026, lot 4): on the fiche's page — ✓ confirm, ✗ reject, ✎ correct, per element, and confirm all (see [[maurice-web-garden]]); a rejected fragment is deleted and never written again, a rejected address never linked again, a rejected relation never proposed again, a fiche Maurice made and the member rejects goes (one the member wrote cannot be rejected); a relation corrected on the page is marked `rewrite`, so the next pass rewrites the pending fragments with it. What the fiches say that is not confirmed is flagged in the chat by the "À vérifier" footer (see [[maurice-chat]]); since 27 September 2026 the same gestures are native in the app (a card under the reply opens the fiche; see [[maurice-chat]]), still writing markdown into the garden. **People found to be one** (27 September 2026, lot 7): two people are joined when the mail says so twice — the same full name (two words at least, any order) and a second strong signal: every address spells the name (`paola.magi@`, `magipaola@`, `pmagi@`, `brosse@`), a thread in common, or two cards of the address book under that name (the body is not kept, so a signature or a phone cannot be one). The name alone joins nothing; a rejected link is never made again. The joined address is pending, `source: guess`, with the reason in `guess`. Before writing, their fiches fold into one: the member's own fiche on that name (with a card of that name) wins and receives a Maurice fiche's fragments and relation; otherwise the absorbed fiches' mail fragments move into the kept one and the fiches Maurice made are removed, their keys forgotten in the store (`documents_record` `forgotten`). Rejecting an address on a fiche splits it out: its mail fragments move to a fiche of its own, pending, keyed on the address. Identities also carry the `names` each address wrote under. First run on the owner's garden: Paola's three fiches folded into his own (Hotmail, pm.me, CPAS), Jonathan Brosse's and Josie Zayner's two into one each; an address she is only written to under a first name (`paola.magi@proton.me`) stays apart. The files the mail pass and the gestures write are pushed to the corpus at once (`indexGardenPaths`), so a fiche is found by a search the same night.

**A confirmed relation is revisited, never rewritten** (28 September 2026). A relation the member confirmed or corrected is theirs, and the pass never writes over it — but it was confirmed on what had been read then: the owner confirmed *Salman sends New Year wishes every 1 January* from Proton the morning before contactoffice was read, where Salman is a colleague. The relation now keeps what it rests on (`relation.basis`: how many of the person's messages, from which mailboxes; for one written earlier, the person's messages up to the newest one it cites). When the person has grown past it — five more messages, or messages from a mailbox the basis did not have — the pass asks the model, with the confirmed relation, whether the new mail changes who the person is; a yes is written beside it under **Maurice proposes** (`relation.proposed` with its sources and dates), a no leaves nothing, and either way the basis moves, so it is asked once per growth, not every night. The member takes it or leaves it on the fiche's page — `POST /api/people/:locale/:basename/review {target: "proposal", action: "confirm" | "reject"}`: accepted, it becomes the relation, confirmed, with its sources; refused, it goes and the relation stays. The review counts it as pending.

Two rules keep this honest. **A fiche is *opened* by a deliberate gesture** — `open_fiche` for a book or a film, writing on it for an article. **An article share is a weak signal**, so the fiche it writes automatically carries `meta.opened: false` until the reader writes on it: a comment at share time or later, a résonance filed on it or sent from it, a highlight with a note. Unopened fiches stay out of the Garden section and out of the résonance target search; the articles shelf still lists them. The absence of the marker means opened, so every fiche written before the rule existed is one.

**The same mark on a note Maurice wrote.** Since 19 September 2026 (P2-C of the domains roadmap) a note can be *written by Maurice, not reviewed yet*: when a member adopts a [[maurice-domains|domain]] and then says yes to notes on it, Maurice writes a hub note and a few topic notes in their garden, each with `meta.opened: false`, `meta.author: maurice`, the domain, the model, the conversations it read (`meta.sources`) and a *Where it comes from* section in the body — the one exception to "Maurice writes only when asked", and it is asked, twice. The mark goes when the member **keeps** the note (the banner's *Keep* in the web garden — `POST /api/v1/garden-tools/review-note` drops the line and commits), **corrects** it (`update_note` with a new body clears it; an edit in Obsidian is theirs to mark), or **throws it away** (delete). The garden tool's `list_notes` says `unreviewed: true` on such a note; the app's garden list shows *to review*. Details in [[maurice-domains]].

## The articles pipeline *(ships)*

One endpoint, `POST /api/v1/garden/articles`, that every capture surface calls: [[maurice-carnet|Carnet]]'s **share sheet**, the **browser clipper** (`clients/web-clipper`), the garden MCP tool. It writes one fiche per article, its full text as a fragment, and its cover. Details that took a while to get right:

- The caller may send the **DOM its browser already rendered**; the server parses that instead of fetching the URL — the only way past a paywall or a bot check. Carnet's share extension runs a script in the Safari page for exactly this.
- **Duplicates** are found by scanning the collection's fiches, under the page's own `rel=canonical` — a tracking link and the plain URL are the same article. A re-share with a comment appends the comment rather than making a twin.
- A site that **refuses robots** (a 403, a bot wall) does not lose the save: a **bookmark** fiche keeps the URL, the comment and the reason under `status: needs-capture`, and a later share from a browser completes it in place. A dead URL is an error, not a bookmark.
- An **AI summary** is generated after the response has gone out, into the frontmatter only, on a file re-read from disk.
- Titles are quoted defensively in YAML — `[Analyse] …` once opened a flow sequence and silently took the fiche out of everything.

Reading them back — list, one article, its full text, highlights, a note — is what Carnet's Articles shelf is built on.

## Highlights & résonances *(ships with Carnet)*

Reading a book chapter or an article in Carnet, you can **highlight** a passage (with a note and a colour; stored in SQLite, per member, anchored to the chapter or the fiche, and to the view — full text or summary — the offsets belong to), and you can file a **résonance**: "this feeds my *Sugar* fiche." A résonance is a dated block written under `## Résonances` on the *target's* working face, with a literal `[[wiki-link]]` back to the source — so Obsidian resolves it natively, computes the backlink and the graph, and the site renders the same syntax. There is no link table; the markdown is the truth.

## Flashcards *(ships, September 2026)*

Cards are generated **on demand**, never on save, from a source: a book chapter, a whole book, a fiche, or one of its fragments. Highlights are not a unit but an input — the passages the reader marked are covered first, and they enter the source hash. One file per pass, beside the fragments, in the syntax of the **Obsidian Spaced Repetition plugin** (`Question::Answer`, multi-line with `?`, reversed with `:::`/`??`, cloze with `==term==`, a `#flashcards/<collection>/<slug>/<unit>` deck line), so the vault reviews the same cards the app does.

The frontmatter carries what the plugin doesn't need: the source, a **hash of it at generation time** (a fiche that has moved on is reported *stale*), the language and mode of the pass, and a **fingerprint of every generated card**. A card whose text no longer matches was edited by hand and survives regeneration; so does one the reader wrote; a regenerated card with the same question keeps its schedule. The schedule is the plugin's own `<!--SR:…-->` comment after each card, written by whichever side reviewed last, with the plugin's SM-2 constants. The `_cards/` directory is **git-ignored** — a review is never a commit, and cards regenerate from their source.

Language is French by default, the fiche's `cards_lang` over that, the call's `lang` / `answer_lang` over both; a *vocabulary* mode makes term-and-meaning cards, both directions, from a text in a language being learned. Asking for cards on a Calibre book with no garden fiche opens one — that request is the deliberate gesture. The server side is complete (`/api/v1/garden/cards`); the Carnet surfaces — a Flashcards line on every media, a review section — are the next tranche.

## Corpus — search across everything *(ships)*

`tools/corpus` is RAG over the household's reading and conversations: `search` (plus `search_by_tags`, `search_by_author`, `search_in_book`, `search_book_summaries`), `get_chunk_context`, `index_conversation` (one conversation, or every one in the background) and `reconcile_status` (how that full pass is going). It moved into the main repo in September 2026 with a test suite, indexes the garden's **notes, fiches, fragments and cards** as they change (the server pushes each write; a watcher inside the gateway catches the rest — moves included since 27 September 2026, which is how an atomic save arrives — and the nightly walks the garden sources as a net under both), and re-embeds only what changed. The vector store is **sqlite-vec**, one file per member under `tools/corpus/data/vectors/` (`<member>.db`, plus `_default.db` for shared content); the Qdrant block in the config is kept only as a migration reference (`specs/corpus-sqlite-vec-migration.md`).

**The embedding model, since 19 September 2026: Qwen3-Embedding, 1024 dimensions.** Until then the corpus ran on `nomic-embed-text`, which does not separate French: mapping Candide's conversations by their vectors ([[maurice-domaines]], step 0) put every French one — 1 173 of them, nearly all of the life lived with Maurice — in one block, whatever the clustering, while the English ones fell into recognisable groups. Three multilingual models were measured on that block with the same protocol: `nomic-embed-text-v2-moe`, `qwen3-embedding:0.6b` and `qwen3-embedding:4b` all separate it into a dozen living domains; Qwen leaves the smallest remainder; the 4B is no better at it than the 0.6B and runs four times slower on the Mac mini (about 4 chunks a second against 19). The decision, and why it is a family rather than one model: **Scaleway serves `qwen3-embedding-8b`** (and `bge-multilingual-gemma2`) and Ollama serves the 0.6B, 4B and 8B, so a household runs whichever size its machine can — **0.6B through Ollama at home, 8B on Scaleway for a hosted instance** — and the family is matryoshka-trained, so every size is asked for the same **1024 dimensions** (`dimensions` on the request; Ollama and Scaleway both honour it, `bge` refuses it) and a store has one shape whichever size wrote it. Never two models in one store, though: `corpus_meta` pins the model and width that wrote a file, and since this change the store **refuses to open** under another (`sqlite_vec_store.py`, `_check_pin`) rather than answering searches from an unrelated space until the next write tripped on the pin. A model change is therefore a full re-index into a fresh store, the old one kept beside it until checked — `python -m src.main index --force` for the file sources, `--source conversations` for the rest; the home corpus (125 000 chunks, 168 M characters, two thirds of it books) takes a night on the 0.6B. Two smaller things came with it. The embedder now knows what each family wants **in front of the text** — Qwen's instruction line on the query and nothing on the document, nomic's `search_query:` / `search_document:`, BGE's markers — so a search is embedded as a query and a chunk as a document; the nomic index had been built with bare text on both sides. And the config is one number: `vector_size` sizes the store and, for a matryoshka family, the request; `query_prefix` / `document_prefix` override the family table when a model needs something else. Hosted households installed before this run `bge-multilingual-gemma2` at its native 3584, set explicitly in their env, and keep it: multilingual too, and a working index is not worth a re-index for the family's sake.

**Live conversations, 19 September 2026.** After every reply the server asks the corpus to reconcile the conversation (`indexConversationInBackground`, fire-and-forget). It called the tool by its bare name, `index_conversation`, while the gateway only knows `corpus__index_conversation`; the gateway answered "Unknown tool" as ordinary text, not as an error, so the call reported success and **no conversation lived after a turn had entered the index since the June import** — three months of Candide's and Paola's talks were invisible to `search` and to anything built on the corpus. The call now goes through `corpusCall`, which carries the prefix and logs a refusal. The backlog was reconciled by hand (`python -m src.main index --source conversations` from `tools/corpus`), and the same day the reconciliation got a schedule: **once a night the server asks the corpus to reconcile every conversation and to prune the file entries whose file is gone** (`services/corpusNightly.ts`, described in [[maurice-server]]) — the safety net the comment in `mcpClient.ts` had been promising. The admin dashboard shows when it last ran and what it did, with a "Reconcile now" button.

It is also the engine behind the **"bring your history"** [[maurice|differentiator]]: `import_chat_export` ingests the official **Claude and ChatGPT** data-export `.zip`s, so your past threads become searchable memory. **Since 19 September 2026 the member does it themselves**, from the Maurice app's settings — *Import my conversations*, one card per provider with where the export comes from, what was synced, the button and the live progress (`ImportConversationsView.swift`) — a setting, deliberately not a step of the onboarding (the domains design, 4f: a slightly technical operation nobody is frightened with; those who do it have a map the next night, the others talk with Maurice for a few weeks and the proposal comes when the matter is there). The routes are the member's own, `POST /api/import?provider=anthropic|chatgpt` (multipart, the `.zip`), `GET /api/import/status?job=`, `GET /api/import/history?provider=` (`routes/import.ts`, `services/chatImport.ts`, shared with the admin console's `/admin/users/:id/import*`); a guest is refused, their life being in another household. Each imported conversation is written with the dates of its export, its `origin`, and — new — **`conversations.imported_at`**, the wall-clock of the import (`chat_import.py`), which is what lets the two nights see it: the mapping reads it like any unbound conversation of the member's, and the briefs, whose `read_until` is a message timestamp older conversations would never pass, compare `imported_at` with the brief's `updated_at` and read the conversation whole once (`services/domainBriefs.ts`; see [[maurice-domains]]). The everyday prompt carries one sentence for a member who never imported — Maurice may say once, when they refer to past conversations with ChatGPT or Claude, that the settings take the export, and never as a pitch — gone once a history is in (`importHintSection`).

**Reachable at last, and in an order, 20 September 2026.** The corpus was an *experimental* family, off by default, which meant it was in none of the owner's conversations: a question landing squarely in one of his domains could not be followed up although the index held the answer. It is now **always on**, with the two guards that being always on requires — never in a room, and only its reading tools — both described in [[maurice-tools]]. The second half of the change is an **order of search**, because the corpus itself has none: the only ranking is the cosine score, every source mixed, and conversations outnumber everything else by a factor of sixty in the owner's index (88 726 chunks against 1 408 notes). Measured on his own store, *"Où en suis-je dans ma pratique du violon ?"* unfiltered returns four conversations and not one note, while a garden note answers it. So the everyday prompt asks each layer separately, one call per layer in the same round (`corpusNotice` in `services/claude.ts`): **the garden** (`source_type` note, fiche, card, fragment) — what the member decided was worth keeping on a subject, which is the promise Maurice makes them — **then what they have already said** (`conversation`), **then what they only read or gathered** (`book`, `dossier`, `thought`). The order is what weighs the results, not what sequences the calls: the first draft did say *one search per step, stop as soon as you have enough*, and on the first real turn the model ignored the sequence and fired both searches in the same round — better on both counts, one round of latency instead of three and every layer answered rather than a walk stopping at the first plausible hit, so the instruction was rewritten to say what the model did. The domain briefs are the entry point: they say what matters, the corpus holds what was actually written (see [[maurice-domains]]). That first turn is also the evidence that the whole thing works: asked about Trump, Maurice searched both layers and brought back a Robert Reich article from the garden that no unfiltered search would ever have surfaced — then, asked what exactly came from the corpus, separated what a search had just returned from what his brief carried, and flagged a date he had estimated rather than read. **The people, a layer of their own** (27 September 2026): whenever a question is about someone — by name or by what they are to the member ("my accountant", "Adriano's violin teacher") — the prompt asks for `{"collection": "people"}` in the same round, and to open the fiche a hit names before searching anywhere else. Measured on the owner's index: "ma comptable" over the garden layer returns the digests of her mail (notes on the accounts) and not her; over the people, her fiche comes first, and "mon prof de violon" finds the violin teacher the same way. The turn's corpus budget went from three searches to four, and a search is a repeat of another only over the same filters — the people layer had been refused as a rewording of the garden's. **And the layers got fast** (the same day): every filtered search extracted the JSON of every chunk — its text included — to test a substring, over the member's 92 000 chunks and the shared pool's 38 000, one to three seconds a search inside the gateway (profiled: disk reads and JSON parsing, the vector distances negligible). `source_type` and `collection` are now matched exactly, lowercased, on expression indexes, and a store file with nothing of the filtered kind (the shared pool, which holds only books) is not searched at all: 0.3 to 0.9 s a search, and the accountant question went from 27 s to 21 s. The first search after a gateway restart builds the indexes once (a few seconds). **The person answers first** (the same afternoon, after "when did I last write to Thomas" went to the mailbox again): the people layer has its own floor — a lookup scores lower than a question, and the general 0.53 was throwing the right fiche away (Thomas at 0.487, the accountant at 0.410); measured on the owner's 140 fiches, the right person scores 0.38–0.54 and a role nobody holds 0.30–0.35, so the floor is 0.37, within 0.06 of the best. A hit in a person's own fiche or fragment carries `person`, written without a model from the fiche: the relation, the addresses, and the exchanges line with the last message — which answers "when did I last write" on its own. And a mail tool called in the same round as a look at the people (a people search, a person's `get_fiche`) is **not run** (`heldForPeople` in `services/claude.ts`): the model reads the fiche and calls it next round if the fiche does not answer. On the email side, `exchanges` with a bare first name matching several addresses returns the candidates rather than their mail mixed, and an address that writes under many other names (a notification relay) is not the person. Measured live: Thomas, one search and 6 s; the accountant, 8 s, no mail tool. `corpus__search`'s own description was corrected at the same time — it listed neither `conversation` nor `card` among the `source_type` values, while conversations are the majority of the index.

**Narrowed before the model reads it, 20 September 2026 (evening).** The index made the prompt small and the searches stayed large: on a second run of the Galápagos turn the prompt was down to 5 655 tokens while the three searches injected about 15 500 — four fifths of the model's reading was what it had gone to fetch. One search weighed **3 233 tokens, of which 1 766 were bookkeeping** (file hash, chunk id, member id, indexing date, embedding model) and six of its ten hits were another passage of a document already in the list. `services/corpusResults.ts` now makes four cuts between the tool and the model: the turn's **own conversation** is dropped, since it came back as its own top two hits and the model holds every word of it already; hits are **grouped by source**, best passage kept; what remains is held to a **floor and to 0.15 below the best hit**, because the corpus has no threshold of its own and answers "Galápagos" with the ten least distant things it owns, ending at *Claude model ID format for opus*; and only the fields a reader can act on survive — source, kind, date, author, passage, score. A search whose every hit is weak comes back **empty**. **Measured: 10 423 → 1 038 tokens across the turn's three searches**, with nothing useful lost — the violin keeps its five best notes and its five conversations, and the Robert Reich article that justified the garden-first order is still there. The source cards are built from the same surviving rows, so what the member sees under the reply is what the model actually read.

**Where the floor is, and how it was found, 21 September 2026.** The floor shipped at **0.35**, a number nobody had measured, and it sat far below the noise: asked what Scoodle, Plantyn and Capture were — three school apps, nothing to do with anything written down — the corpus answered with April's agendas and a four-year-old conversation about dreams, every hit between 0.411 and 0.514, all of them above 0.35 and so all of them drawn as cards under the reply. `server/scripts/corpus-floor.ts` is the bench that settled it: sixteen questions through the gateway, eight the corpus holds an answer to and eight it does not, each against both layers, every pair labelled **on the title of its best hit** rather than on the question — the garden holds nothing on the Galápagos while the conversations do, so one question is a real hit on one layer and noise on the other. On Qwen3-Embedding 0.6B at 1024 dimensions the two families do not overlap: **a real answer scores 0.540 to 0.782** (the lowest being *US Politics and Corruption*, asked which books on American democracy), **noise scores 0.389 to 0.516** (the highest being *Itinéraire du voyage en Chine*, asked how to change a timing belt). The floor is now **0.53**, in the gap. Two things that came out of the measurement: the conversation layer scores systematically higher than the garden, noise included (0.46–0.52 against 0.39–0.45), and a floor per layer still buys nothing since one floor above both separates them already; and the gap is **0.024 wide** on one member's index, so the bench, not the number, is the thing to keep — re-run it after an embedding-model change, since the scale moves with the model.

## Facts of a life — what Maurice may know about you *(ships, 20 September 2026)*

A third kind of memory, beside the domain briefs and the corpus. A brief describes a live subject and is rewritten nightly; a corpus hit is a dated passage of something that was said. Neither fits *"Emilio is eleven"* — small, stable, true until it changes. The measurement that asked for it: on the Galápagos turn Maurice went searching the corpus for the children's ages, about a thousand tokens spent on something he should simply have known.

**He writes one down when he learns it**, during the conversation, through the native `remember_fact` tool (`services/lifeFacts.ts`). **But he proposes it, he does not know it.** The card goes out in the reply itself, and the fact reaches no prompt until the member has kept it. That order is the whole design: a model writing unsupervised into someone's memory eventually writes something false into it, and the member is the one who has to live with that. Kept facts sit in the everyday prompt directly under the member's own `profile_text`, which stays theirs, written by hand — these are held apart so they can be taken back one at a time.

Three guards, chosen before they were needed rather than after: **two facts per turn** at most, counted across the turn's rounds; **200 characters** per fact, since anything longer is a brief in disguise and belongs to a domain; and the same sentence is never proposed twice — unless it was dismissed, because people change their minds. A refusal is an ordinary answer the model reads and learns from within the turn, not an error to retry.

**Judged twice, by one model rather than by whoever is speaking.** The tool is called by the model holding the conversation, which changes from thread to thread — DeepSeek Flash on one turn, GLM Flash on the next, Opus sometimes — and whether a sentence is a lasting fact or this week's project is a judgement that should not depend on that. Every proposal therefore passes a second time, through the model pinned to the **`life_fact`** ancillary invocation (standard tier, recommended on the night models, changeable in the console): one line, `KEEP` or `DROP`, and it may reword a clumsy sentence — that wording is then what the member is shown. It runs in the member's turn and is charged to them, like seeding a domain. The refusals that cost nothing are made before it is paid (empty, too long, already known, over the two-per-turn cap). And it **fails open**: a judge that breaks, or that answers neither word, lets the proposal through — the member is the real gate, and a second opinion being down must not stop Maurice learning. *(The two-per-turn cap did not actually hold until 21 September 2026: the counter was created once per turn in all three model loops and never handed to the tool, so every call read zero and the cap never closed. It is passed now, alongside the turn's search budget, which is what found it.)*

The member keeps, corrects, dismisses or forgets, from the card in the transcript or through `/api/life-facts` (`routes/lifeFacts.ts`). A dismissed fact stays on file, marked, so the same sentence does not come back next week. Forgetting deletes it outright, including one kept for months. The card (`LifeFactCard.swift`) is deliberately two buttons and no menu: the decision is small and should cost one tap.

## Tracks — research *(experimental)*

`tools/tracks` turns a question into structured output: **deep research** (`request_deep_research`, `get_research_status`), **briefings** (`generate_briefing`, `get_latest_briefing`), **dossiers** (`get_dossier`, `get_dossier_tree`, `list_dossiers`), and **signal reports** (`trigger_signal_report`). Findings can flow into the garden via `publish_content`. Storage is SQLite.

## Ships vs. exists

**Garden, the articles pipeline, résonances, flashcards, and corpus ship.** **Tracks are experimental** (the private `maurice-tools` overlay).

## Gaps & notes

- **No automatic "temporal mirror."** Daily notes are a real, deliberate garden artifact, but Maurice does **not** generate weekly/monthly reviews of your thinking — that idea has no code (see [[maurice|the vision's]] corrected scope).
- **Flashcards have no backup.** The gardens rely on git, which ignores the cards; the nightly backup covers `maurice.db` only. Either the backup grows to cover `~/.maurice/gardens`, or the cards are accepted as regenerable.
- **The corpus reconciles itself nightly** (closed 19 September 2026, `services/corpusNightly.ts`): every conversation and a prune per member, from 03:00 local, once a day, and the admin can run it now. Since the evening of 19 September 2026 it also sweeps the shared `_default.db` (books, dossiers) with `corpus__prune {shared: true}`, so a book removed from Calibre loses its chunks the next night. Since 27 September 2026 it also walks the garden sources (`corpus__reindex` on notes, fiches, cards, fragments; only what changed is embedded): the watcher ignored moves, so every note saved atomically — by an editor, git or a coding session — had stayed out of the index, 31 of the owner's among them, and a file changed while the gateway was down was never caught either. Both now land by the next night at the latest.
- **The shared pool is on the old model until the books are re-indexed.** The re-index of 19 September 2026 runs conversations, gardens, then books, in that order; until the last source is done, the fresh store answers without the books, and the old store is kept beside it (`tools/corpus/data-nomic-embed-text/`) for a rollback.
- **Tracks' "deep research" depth** is the least battle-tested of the three; treat it as experimental.
- **The import's job lives in the gateway's memory** (`import_status` answers "unknown job" after a gateway restart, though the run is logged in `import_history.db` and the conversations are in); and the app reads the whole `.zip` into memory before uploading it — fine for a Claude export, to be watched on a multi-hundred-megabyte ChatGPT one (the server side already accepts them).
