# Carnet — the pocket companion

**Carnet** is the second native client, in its own repo. Where the Maurice app is the conversational front door and the [[maurice-web-garden|web garden]] is the browsable read surface, Carnet is the pocket client for *capturing and consuming* a household's signals and content: log what happened, read and edit what's remembered. Since 8 October 2026 it wears **the Maurice app's theme** (one warm ink on a warm paper, no accent colour, Young Serif for titles, rounded shapes; the Grid theme it had before is retired) with Liquid Glass on everything that floats (see below), pairs to the same [[maurice-server|server]], and uses the same household identity — pair once, switch members with a PIN — so login and visual language are consistent across every surface.

Started in May 2026; iOS 17+, XcodeGen project, SwiftUI.

## Three peer roles

| Role | What it does | Backed by |
|---|---|---|
| **Log** | Fast ad-hoc capture — meals, exercise, breathwork, meditation, notes, documents, tasks — via a radial FAB, free-text parsing and the share extension. | `signals`, `uploads` |
| **Dashboards** | Server-defined layouts of warm-monochrome widgets over the household's health, activity, and coaching data, navigable by day. | `layouts`, health, `signals`, `coaching` |
| **Content** | Read and edit the household's living memory: reading (books and articles), the garden's entries, résonances, what Maurice keeps on the member (the domains and their briefs, the conversations he opens), and — next — flashcards. | `calibre`, `garden/*`, `domains`, `conversations` |

## Entries: one list, two faces

Since 8 October 2026 the home is **one list of entries of every kind** — books, articles, films, series, music, podcasts, games, posts — read from the server's shelf (`garden/entries?view=shelf`, [[maurice-server]]). It replaces, as the root, the Books / Articles / Domains shelves, and in the minimal scope the Garden section. An entry has up to three parts, and the app is built on telling them apart:

- **the source** — someone else's words, readable here: a book of the household's Calibre library, an article's captured text. A film, a series, a book read on paper have none;
- **my side** — what the member keeps for themself: the fiche's prose, dated notes and quotes, résonances, the passages highlighted while reading, what Maurice filed;
- **the shared side** — what the member wrote to be read: the body of the card, a *draft* until *published*.

"Fiche" and "card" are the names in the code and in the garden; the app shows neither word.

**The list** (`Views/Shelf/ShelfView.swift`). A row says, under the title, what the member has on it — *3 notes*, a hollow ring for *Draft*, a solid disc for *Published*, or *Not opened* — and, at the right, only when there is something to read, where they are in it (a percentage and a bar for a book, minutes for an article, *Link only* for a bookmark the site refused). The kinds are ticked in the floating panel at the bottom, several at once, each with its count; the same panel offers to resume the book being read, which opens the reader straight at the member's position. Search and sort are in the capsule top-right. A kind the build has never heard of is left out rather than failing the list. The last list seen is kept for offline. **Swiping a row** (9 October 2026): to the right puts it away from the list (*Archive*), to the left deletes it (*Delete*, after a confirmation that says what leaves the garden, whether a page comes off the site, and that a book stays in the library); both act on release once the row is pulled past a threshold. A book of the library with no entry can be put away and not deleted. *Archived*, in the member's menu, shows the other half of the list, where the same swipe to the right brings a row back. The list is a `LazyVStack`, not a `List`, so the gesture is written by hand (`SwipeRow`), beside the scroll view's own. The row is opened by a tap that `SwipeRow` owns, not by a `NavigationLink` inside it: a button fires on any touch that ends within it, however far the finger went sideways, and the first version opened the entry at the end of every swipe (a long-press menu went for the same reason, its preview rising while the finger dragged). Checked in the simulator on a bench of plain rows: right archives, left deletes, a side with no action does nothing, a tap opens, scrolling triggers nothing. See [[maurice-server]] for what the two actions do.

**A page's own picture, read on the phone** (10 October 2026, `LinkCover` in `Shared/CarnetAPI.swift`). From Safari a share carries the rendered page, and the server reads its text and its sharing picture from it. From any other app it carries an address, the server fetches, and many sites refuse it: the article was saved bare. The share extension now asks the system's link preview (`LPMetadataProvider`, what draws the previews in Messages: a real web engine, on the phone's own connection) for the page's title and picture as soon as it has the address, while the member writes their note; when the save comes back without a picture it sends the one it found (`PUT …/cover`), scaled to 1200 px through ImageIO, within ten seconds or not at all. The same call serves articles already saved, from *Refresh details*. It brings the picture and the title, not the text: an article the server could not read stays a bookmark until it is saved from Safari. Run on the Mac against a refusing site; not yet watched in the extension on a phone. **Opened from outside** (9 October 2026). Carnet answers `carnet://entry/<collection>/<locale>/<slug>`: the list reads itself again if it has not heard of the entry, and pushes it. The Maurice app sends its member here from what a conversation offered them to keep ([[maurice-chat]]). The link names no household: it opens in the one on screen, and does nothing when the entry is not in it. Minimal scope only — the full scope's drawer does not route it.

**What is not an entry** is under the member's button, top-left: a short menu with *Entries*, *Archived*, *Notes* and *Maurice* (the domains and the conversations he opened, described below), and member and household switching at its head.

**Every note** (`Views/Shelf/NotesView.swift`, 9 October 2026). The list finds a note through what it was written on, and its search reads titles and authors only; a note remembered for itself — roughly when, roughly what, the article long forgotten — had no way in. *Notes* is that way: everything the member wrote, whatever the entry, newest first under a heading per month, each note drawn as on its entry's page with the entry named beneath in small (cover, title, kind), and a tap opens the entry. A note here is the member's own words — a dated note, a quote copied, a résonance, a highlight written under; a bare highlight, a fiche's prose and what Maurice filed stay on the entry's page. Notes on an archived entry are listed all the same: the row was put away, not what was written on it. The magnifier searches the notes' text, their quotes and the entries' titles, every word typed, accents and case aside. Read from the server each time (`garden/entries?view=notes`, [[maurice-server]]); nothing is kept for offline.

**The entry** (`Views/Shelf/EntryView.swift`). Identity on top: cover, title, kind and author, rating, status and date. Then the source, when there is one, as a sunken band that is not paper — *Resume* or *Read* as its primary action, *Chapters* (the book's page) or *Original* at its corner. **An article opens on its summary** instead (19 October 2026): the essence in a paragraph, and under it *See more…*, which unfolds the long summary meant to be read in the article's place — its thesis, arguments, figures and conclusion, in a few paragraphs whose length follows the article's. The step between the essence and the whole text was what was missing. Both are asked for, while the page shows what there is, when one is missing; the reader's *Summary* view reads the long one. Its fiche is then a screen of its own, *Fiche* beside *Read* on the source band, with how much is on it. Otherwise, below the source, **one sheet with two faces**: my side on the palette's paper sown with dots, the shared side on smooth paper in a reading serif. The sheet's bottom corner is folded over, shows the paper of the other face and what is there (*Draft*, *Published*, *Blank*, *3 notes*), and is how the sheet is turned over. An entry with nothing on the member's side opens on its shared side. Citations in the shared text (`[[wiki-links]]`) are underlined and tappable when the entry they name has a published page, dotted when it is the member's alone.

**Writing.** The panel at the bottom follows the face that is up. *My side*: a dotted field opens the note sheet — text, and optionally the passage it is about and a page — and the note lands as a dated block on the fiche; a book of the library with no entry yet gets one from its first note. *Shared side*: *Write* when it is blank; otherwise a pencil, the page's address, and *Publish* for a draft, with a confirmation, after which the panel says two things apart (since 9 October 2026): *Published · going online…* as soon as the server has answered, then *Online* for a few seconds once the site carries the page, then its address (*Published, but not online* when the deploy failed; the same the other way for *Unpublish*). It asks the server every two seconds, a publish being about fifteen; The ⋯ menu opens on every entry and offers the same things for every kind (until 10 October 2026 it was disabled whenever the face that was up had no file — a podcast, an article kept as a link — and so did nothing at all); it falls back on the other face's file, and shows a spinner while it works. *Unpublish* is in the ⋯ menu, beside *Refresh details* (10 October 2026), which completes the card from what the fiche knows of its subject — cover, author or platform, the date of its kind ([[maurice-server]]) — and, for an article that shows without a picture, reads the page's own (below) — with the links to the files (garden site, Working Copy, Obsidian). *Publish* only shows to a member who has somewhere to publish to — a domain of their own, or the household's public pages. In the reader, *Note* writes on the book's entry, with the chapter's name.

## An entry shared with other members *(11 October 2026)*

A media entry can be shared with members of the household, the way a conversation is. At the top of the entry, left of ⋯: the badges of who is on it, and a button to add someone; both open the same sheet — who is there, and the household's members who are not. The fiche stays **one file in its owner's garden**: nothing is copied. A member it is shared with finds it in their own list under the owner's badge (its id opens on `@<owner>/`), reads all of it — the notes written before the share too — and adds notes of their own.

- **Signed notes.** A note by someone other than the owner is written `DATE — @username — text` (or `DATE — @username — p. 12 :` for a block); a note that names nobody is the owner's. On a shared entry each item opens on its author's badge. A note kept from a conversation with Maurice shows Maurice's mark with the badge of whoever started that conversation over its corner; what Maurice filed himself shows his mark alone. An entry nobody shares keeps its plain look.
- **Rights.** The owner adds and removes; a member may only take themselves off. A member's one write is a note: every other write named at someone else's entry (`?owner=`) is refused, so it can never land on an entry of the same slug in their own garden. Adding follows the conversation rules — a guest's allow-list, blocks.
- **What stays each member's own.** Highlights and the reading position (per member, in SQLite), and the conversations themselves, which have their own participants. The owner's card — the public side — is not part of the share.
- Server: `entry_shares` ([[maurice-data-model]]), `data-api/services/entryShares.ts`, `POST`/`DELETE …/entries/:c/:l/:s/participants`, and `?owner=<username>` on the entry's page and its notes.

## Maurice, from an entry *(9 October 2026; in the Maurice app since 19 October)*

On the member's side the panel carries, beside *Add a note…*, a **Maurice** button. Since 19 October 2026 it opens the conversation **in the Maurice app** (`Views/Maurice/MauriceHandoff.swift`): with no conversation about the entry yet, a new one directly; otherwise a short menu — *New conversation* first, then the ones already held about it, newest first. A new one is held *from* the entry, its fiche loaded as context by the server ([[maurice-chat]]); a second tap before anything was said there reopens the same empty one. An entry can so be talked about many times, in different settings, and each conversation's result lands in the fiche separately (below). The article reader's Maurice button does the same.

**On the fiche, the conversations** (19 October 2026). The member's side lists the conversations held about the entry — from its page, or linked to it in the Maurice app — one line each: title, day, setting, number of messages, and an arrow that opens it in the Maurice app. Under each line, **what it came to**, without asking: the fiche's own fragment, written by the server once the conversation has settled (twenty minutes after its last message) and rewritten when it goes on — keyed on the hash of the transcript it read. Before that, the line says so and offers *Write it now*; a summary the conversation has outgrown says it will be updated; one that came out wrong is written again with *Rewrite*. The article's summary card has its own *Rewrite*, which writes both summaries again from the article's text. A link to a conversation in a kept note opens it in the Maurice app too.

**Without the Maurice app on the device**, the thread is held in Carnet as it was before (`Views/Maurice/AskMauriceView.swift`): a full screen with the entry's title, a field (typed or dictated), and Maurice's reply as it is written, made at the first question, not at the tap.

A conversation is rarely worth keeping; its result is. So under each reply that thread shows what the server found worth keeping, on the dotted paper it will land on: the other works and people the exchange touched — no longer a note for the entry itself (19 October 2026: the conversation's summary on its fiche says that) (*Keep and link*, *Add and link* when the member already has them; a pick first when the identity is unsure; *Search again* and *Refine* under each when the lookup found the wrong work, and for a podcast the show's episodes with the one the conversation was about picked in advance, as in the app — 14 October 2026). An episode on the shelf reads under its show's name. A kept note's link to its conversation is its own line under the note, not an inline grey link; a `carnet://entry/…` link is resolved by name, retried while the app wakes, rather than looked for once in the list; an entry shows the cover `open_fiche` downloaded when its text names a provider's address. Keeping files the note; for another entry it also links the two, by a résonance on this one. The note ends on a link to the conversation, and a tap on it in the entry's page opens that conversation in the same screen, with what was kept of it. Carnet keeps no socket: once the reply is in it asks `GET /api/suggestions?conversation=…&settle=1`, which answers when the pass the reply started is done. Closing the screen re-reads the entry if anything was kept. A book of the library nothing is written on has no Maurice button: it is not an entry until a first note.

**Dictation** (the same day). A microphone sits beside every field Carnet writes in — the note sheet, the shared side's editor, the question to Maurice (`Views/DictationMic.swift`). The engine is the Maurice app's, copied as is (`Services/Dictation.swift`, see [[maurice-chat]]): since 16 October 2026 Apple's SpeechAnalyzer on the device where the system has it (iOS 26), the household's server (Whisper) first by default — Settings › *Transcribe on the household server*, which the member can turn off — and the old recogniser last — through Apple's servers only after the member said yes, once. From the server, an on-device preview is written as the member speaks and Whisper's text replaces it at the stop. Apple's model is fetched when a mic appears; a first wait on it opens a note over the mic with its progress. On the device it listens in **the entry's language**, not the phone's; Whisper hears which language was spoken by itself. What is heard is appended to the field and stays editable; nothing is kept or sent on the member's behalf.

## Reading

*Since 8 October 2026 the shelves below are no longer the home: a book or an article is reached from the list of entries, and its own page (chapters, summaries, offline copy) from its entry's source band. What follows describes those pages and the readers, which are unchanged in what they do.*

The **Library** shows the household's Calibre books with covers, chapters, summaries, and where you left off, ordered by what you last opened and then by what Calibre took in most recently (`/books` carries the Calibre `timestamp` as `added`; the last-opened side comes from the on-device reading positions, so the order is per device until a position syncs), with a sort menu for **Recently added**, **Title** and **Author**; the reader restores the exact position (character index in the full text, paragraph in the summary), synced per member and kept offline. That sync only started working in September 2026: `reading_progress` came from akita keyed on `book_id` alone, `member_id` was added by an ALTER that cannot change a primary key, and the upsert's `ON CONFLICT(member_id, book_id)` therefore threw on every call — silently, with the on-device store covering for it. The table is now rekeyed on the pair, so two members can track the same book and a position can reach the server. It is also what the [[maurice-composer|composer]]'s `progress` book scope reads. A row with `enabled = 0` is paused and the reader no longer moves it — which is how you consult a chapter well ahead without it counting as read. A book's page keeps Resume in a pinned footer and everything else behind one glyph each, two of which open a drawer: **reading** (where you are, a switch that pauses tracking so consulting an index or a chapter further on does not count as read, and reset) and **this book** (offline copy, summaries). Chapters can be extracted and summarised from there — by the server itself since 15 October 2026, so on a hosted household too, the summaries written by the household's default model (see [[maurice-architecture]]); a book can be downloaded for offline reading. Anything that places the reader goes by chapter *slug*: `Chapter.index` is derived from the file's `NNNN-` prefix and counts the front matter the list hides, so using it put the reader several chapters ahead of where they were. Passages can be **highlighted** with a note and a colour.

The article reader shows the page's headings, bold and italic (the `styles` of [[maurice-knowledge]]'s articles pipeline), and its heading — publication, title, reading time — is the first thing in the text's flow rather than a band pinned above it (11 October 2026; the book reader keeps its pinned heading). Its bottom panel is the entry page's own — *Add a note…* and Maurice's mark, one shared view — and the Text / Summary switch sits at the top, centred between back and the type size; the entry's page reads itself again each time it reappears, so what was written in the reader is on it. The book reader keeps its own panel (progress, chapters, switch, note). An article's text is cached on the device under its `text_rev` (11 October 2026): when the server cleans or retakes the text, the copy here is fetched again. The **Articles** shelf lists what was saved through the share sheet or the browser clipper, straight from the garden, each row carrying the lead image the save captured (served open at `/api/garden-images/<member>/…`, the mirror of the site's session-gated `/images/…`; a bookmark the site refused has none and keeps a lettered placeholder): readable in place when the text was captured, marked as a bookmark when the site refused, with the AI summary, highlights, notes, and links to the original and to the fiche on the site. An article that nobody has written on shows as *not a fiche yet*, with a button to open it by writing a note. See the pipeline in [[maurice-knowledge]].

## Domains

*Since 8 October 2026 this screen is called* Maurice *and is reached from the member's menu rather than as a third shelf; in the full scope the Domains shelf below is as it was.*

Since 19 September 2026 the reading section has a third shelf beside Books and Articles, **Domains** — what Maurice keeps on the member ([[maurice-domains]]), which is read and corrected here the way a book is read: the same toggle, the same floating chrome, the same member badge. The shelf lists, in this order, the conversations Maurice opened on his own (*Opened by Maurice*, with *Unread* until the member opens one), the member's **domains** (each row with when its brief was last written and by whom — *Written by Maurice · 3d*, *In your words · 3d*, *No brief yet*; a guest sees *Shared with you* and no page) and their **reading companions** (each leading to its book). A mark on the grid stands where the Maurice app draws its own: a closed book, open pages, a speech bubble filled while unread.

A domain opens its **page** (`Views/Domains/DomainBriefView.swift`): the name and tagline, the status line (who wrote the brief, when, from how many conversations), the brief in an editor that grows with the text, and the actions — **Save** (`PUT /api/domains/:id/brief`; what the member wrote is what Maurice reads from the next turn on; an empty text erases), **Rewrite now** (`POST …/brief/refresh`, the outcome said in one line: rewritten from N conversations, nothing new, allowance spent, failed), **Erase** (`DELETE`, with a confirmation). There is no *Talk about it* and no *Edit*: Carnet has no chat, and the domain itself (statement, context, model, tools) is edited in the Maurice app.

A conversation Maurice opened is **read** here (`Views/Domains/OpenedConversationView.swift`): the title, the date, each message with its author — his, and the member's if they already answered from the Maurice app — its markdown read for its inline marks. Opening it marks it read (`POST /api/conversations/:id/read`), as the Maurice app does. Answering is the Maurice app's: *Reply in Maurice* opens `maurice://conversations/<id>` when a build of the Maurice app answers that scheme (Carnet declares `maurice` in `LSApplicationQueriesSchemes`); until then a line says the conversation is waiting there under the same title.

On a book's page, the *This book* drawer says whether Maurice follows the book — *Followed by Maurice · JTTW guide · conversation in the Maurice app* — and, when he does not, offers **Follow**: a row of `maurices` of kind `companion` bound to the book at the reading position (`POST /api/maurices`, `kind: companion`, the book on the composer's `progress` scope). The companion begins on the book's page; its pinned conversation lives in the Maurice app.

**Push.** Carnet keeps no socket to the server, so what it must hear about comes as an APNs push — today, the conversation Maurice opens ([[maurice-chat]]). Once a member is signed in the app asks for notifications, registers the device (`aps-environment` in the entitlements) and hands the token to **every paired household** as `POST /api/users/me/device-token` with `platform: carnet-ios` and the local household id as `household_tag` (`Services/CarnetNotifications.swift`); a tap on the notification switches to the household it came from and opens the conversation, on a cold start too (the notification-center delegate is set before launch ends). The [[maurice-server|server]] still sends every token under the Maurice app's topic (`APNS_TOPIC`): APNs answers `DeviceTokenNotForTopic` for a Carnet token until `apns.ts` picks the topic by platform — the one server change P3-C left behind.

The domains shelf is the same in both scopes; the API lives in `Services/DomainsAPI.swift` (`GET /api/domains`, the brief routes, `GET /api/conversations`, `POST /api/maurices`, the device token).

## The share extension

"Save to Carnet" takes a URL, a file, or data from any app and posts it as the active member. For a web page it runs a script inside the Safari page and hands back the **rendered DOM**, so an article is saved from what you actually read rather than from a server-side fetch that meets a paywall. The sheet shows the page's title and a *Save* button; the note field is folded behind an *Add a note* link (it opens by itself when the share carries a highlighted passage), and there is no tags field since 11 October 2026. A note typed in the sheet becomes the fiche's first comment — and the gesture that opens it. The send finishes before the sheet dismisses, and an offline queue keeps shares that couldn't reach the server.

## Garden

*In the minimal scope the list of entries has taken this section's place; it remains, as described, in the full scope's drawer.*

The **Garden** section lists every entry of the member's garden across all collections, filterable by type and searchable, ordered by what was last written on (`/garden/entries` carries `updated_at`, the newest mtime across an entry's faces) with a sort menu for the entry's own date and for title; each entry opens its card or its fiche where it lives — in the browser, or in Obsidian on the Mac and Working Copy on the iPad, which hold the same checkout the server writes to. Rows carry their cover art where the entry has some; a note's illustration does not show, since it lives under `images/notes` and the open mirror only serves `images/resources` — a private note's artwork is not worth an unauthenticated route. Unopened article fiches stay out of this list. From a book, an article, or a highlighted passage, **Résonance** files a dated `[[wiki-link]]` block on another entry's fiche.

## Design: the Maurice theme, and Liquid Glass

Carnet takes its colours from the Maurice app's palettes (`CarnetTheme.swift` mirrors `MauriceTheme.swift`: seven light, five dark, Cream by day and Studio by night; ink and hairlines derive from the palette). There is **no accent colour**: the one primary action of a screen is a capsule in inverted ink, a state is a shape (ring, disc) and not a hue, and the default highlight is a wash of ink. Titles are in Young Serif, text in the system font, eyebrows in system mono. Content stays flat on the paper; the *chrome* — what floats over the content — takes **Liquid Glass** on iOS 26 and, before, the same rounded shapes in the palette's surface under a hairline, the deployment target staying at iOS 17. `CarnetApp/Views/Glass.swift` holds the split, the same shape as the Maurice app's: every helper is behind `#available(iOS 26)` with the pre-26 rendering beside it, and an environment flag (`glassChrome`) tells a control it sits inside glass, where it drops its hairline square and renders as a bare glyph. The rule follows Apple's adoption guide — glass on the functional layer only, sparingly, never on content:

- **Floating chrome.** No screen uses the system navigation bar; each pins its own row over the scrolling content (`safeAreaBar` on 26, so the scroll edge effect keeps the controls legible; a painted `safeAreaInset` before): back or the member badge alone at the leading edge, the actions together in one capsule at the trailing edge (`GlassBar`), the title and the shelf toggle scrolling beneath. Shelves, book and article details, both readers, both highlights lists.
- **Search** lives in the bottom bar: a magnifier alone that morphs (`glassEffectID` in a `GlassEffectContainer`) into a full-width field with a close button — iOS 26's minimized search behaviour. The glass sits on the *button*, not its label; on the label the hit area drifted off the glyph.
- **Glass footers.** The book page's Resume footer and the readers' panel (where you are, *Text / Summary*, previous and next chapter, *Note*) float as inset glass panels (`glassFooter`), the full-width material strip before.
- **Sheets** keep the system's glass background (`glassSheetBackground` paints nothing on 26, the page background before); the PIN and invite cards are glass without a drop shadow; the member picker's system buttons take `.glass` / `.glassProminent`; the reset-progress confirmation is attached to its button so it originates from it.
- **Not glass:** rows, covers, cards, the shelf toggle, progress bars and text — the content stays flat on the paper. *The book's page, the article's page and the Domains rows still carry square boxes from the Grid: they took the new colours and type, not yet the new shapes.*

A DEBUG launch argument, `-carnet.previewReading`, walks the reading screens on the bundled samples without a server or a member, which is how the chrome is checked on iOS 26 and iOS 18 simulators.

## Scope and distribution

Carnet has two scopes, chosen at compile time by `CarnetScope.current` (`CarnetApp/CarnetScope.swift`). **Full** is the drawer with the three roles above. **Minimal** — the cut that ships to TestFlight since 11 September 2026, for the two-person experiment — is reading only: the Books, Articles and (since 19 September) Domains shelves, the reader, the fiches, the domain pages and the conversations Maurice opens, the share extension that feeds the articles, plus household and member switching. The domains are in the minimal scope on purpose: a brief is what Maurice keeps on you, made readable, and reading and correcting it is reading — nothing of the Log or Dashboards roles comes with it. A fresh device joins a household the way the Maurice app does, with an **invite code** (`/api/auth/enroll`, then the member sets a PIN): the admin password is only for the owner. In the minimal scope there is no drawer: the list of entries is the root (the library was, until 8 October 2026) and the active member's badge, alone at the leading edge of the floating chrome where ☰ used to be, opens the member/household sheet (households, members, *Add household*, *Disconnect*, and since 14 October 2026 a *Privacy* section: the privacy policy, the terms of use, and **Delete my account…**, which calls `POST /api/me/erase` with scope `account` — the username typed again, the password or PIN asked again — the same contract as the Maurice app's *My data*). Nothing is deleted; the other sections come back by flipping the value. **Location is gone from the app** (14 October 2026): the tracking screen (`LocationView`, `LocationService`) and the places editor (`PlacesView`) were reachable from no screen in either scope, yet `Info.plist` still declared the background-location mode and an *Always* permission worded for akita — App Review would have refused the build. Code and keys were removed; they are in git history if the Log role ever wants them back. The app and the share extension carry a privacy manifest (`PrivacyInfo.xcprivacy`: `UserDefaults`, CA92.1 and 1C8F.1 for the App Group; no tracking, nothing collected by the publisher).

**TestFlight.** `./build-testflight.sh` at the repo root archives the `Carnet` scheme for iOS and uploads it, the same circuit as the Maurice app: marketing version from `./VERSION` (kept at `1.0.0` so external builds ship without a new beta review), build number asked of App Store Connect by `asc-build-info.ts`, signed with the company team's API key (`PROVISIONING_PROFILE_IOS` names the App Store profile, `Carnet iOS App Store`; the share extension uses the one of the same name ending in ` Share`). ## Ships vs. exists

Carnet is in daily use at home; the minimal scope is on its way to TestFlight (1.0.0 on the company team's record since 13 October 2026, internal testing only; build 3, uploaded 14 October 2026, is the one prepared for App Review — no location, account deletion and legal links in the member sheet, privacy manifests; external testers and the App Store both wait for Apple's review). Its reading and garden features depend on the Calibre and garden routes of the [[maurice-server|data-api]]; the domains shelf on the domains, conversations and device-token routes of the chat engine (core, ships); the Log and Dashboards roles depend on the experimental [[maurice-life|life]] tools.
