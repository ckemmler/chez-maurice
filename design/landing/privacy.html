Privacy Policy

Last updated: 15 October 2026

The short version. Maurice can live in two places. On a server you run yourself, your data stays there and we never see it. On a household we host for you, your data is kept on servers we operate in Paris, to run the service and for nothing else: we do not sell it, we do not use it for advertising or to track you, and the apps carry no analytics.

Who we are

Chez Maurice, the Maurice app (iPhone, iPad, Mac), the Carnet app (iPhone, iPad) and the hosted households at *.chezmaurice.eu are published and operated by Palacehotel Software SRL, Belgium (“we”, “us”). For a hosted household, we are the controller of the data described below. Contact: bonjour@chezmaurice.eu.

What the apps connect to

Both apps talk to one place: the Maurice server of the household you joined, at the address you entered or that your invitation named. That is either a server run by you or someone you know, or a household we host. The apps send nothing to us otherwise. They contain no analytics, advertising, tracking or third-party telemetry SDKs, and they do not track you across other companies’ apps or websites.

On a server you run yourself

Everything — conversations, notes, files, books, mail you let Maurice read — is stored on that server, under the control of whoever runs it (the “operator”). We have no access to it and receive nothing from it. The operator decides which AI providers it uses and is responsible for it; this policy describes how the software behaves, not what an operator does with it.

On a household we host

We store, on servers rented from Scaleway in Paris (France), what the household needs to work:

  • Your account: at sign-up, the household’s name, a first name and an email address; members’ names, avatars, roles and hashed PINs or passwords. When payment is in place, it is handled by Stripe: we never see your card number.
  • What you create: conversations with Maurice and with other members, your notes (your “garden”), entries and highlights, the files and images you upload, the books in the household library and your reading positions.
  • Your mail, only if you connect a mailbox: the mailbox password, sealed with a key specific to your household and never shown to anyone; the headers of your messages (sender, recipients, date; subjects are sealed); and, only after you say yes, what Maurice reads in the messages and the notes he writes from them in your garden. Message bodies stay in your mailbox. Address books you connect are read the same way.
  • What it costs: for each reply, the model used and its cost, so that the household’s budget can be kept.
  • Technical logs: requests, timings and errors, kept on the server to keep it running.

Backups of a hosted household are encrypted and kept on Scaleway’s storage in France for up to twelve months. A paused household keeps its data so that it can resume where it stopped.

We do not read your conversations, notes or mail. The people who run the service can technically reach the servers; that access is used only to keep the service running and secure, at your request, or when the law requires it.

AI models and other services

  • Models. To answer, the server sends the conversation’s content, and what you chose to load into it, to an AI model. On a hosted household that is Scaleway’s Generative APIs, in Paris, which process it to answer and do not keep it to train models. On a server you run, it is the provider its operator configured (for example Anthropic, OpenAI, Mistral or Scaleway), or a local model.
  • Background work — summaries of books and articles, what Maurice keeps on the parts of your life he follows, reading your mail when you asked — goes to the same kind of model, in your household’s name.
  • Web search, when enabled, sends the search query to a search provider. Catalogue lookups (a book’s cover, a film’s year) send the title looked up, not your notes.
  • Push notifications: Apple gives your device a token, which the app registers with your household’s server so that Apple’s Push Notification service can deliver notifications.

On your device

The apps keep your session credentials in the system Keychain, and your preferences and offline copies (books, articles, lists) in their own storage. Dictation runs on the device when your language has an on-device model; otherwise it uses Apple’s speech service only after you allow it. Carnet does not use your location.

Who else sees your content

Your private conversations and your garden are yours. What you post in a shared room is seen by the members of that room; a note or an entry you share is seen by the members you shared it with; a note you publish is public. Messages reported in a shared room go to the household’s operator for moderation.

Children

A household is set up by an adult, who may add children as members. A child has no public pages and Maurice proposes nothing to them on his own. Their data is the household’s, under the adult who runs or holds it.

Your rights

In the Maurice app, Settings → My data lets you export everything your household holds about you, erase it, or delete your account; Carnet offers Delete my account in its member sheet. Erasure reaches everything on the server; encrypted backups age out within twelve months. You can also write to us to access, correct, export or delete your data, or to close a hosted household. You may complain to the Belgian Data Protection Authority (dataprotectionauthority.be).

Changes
